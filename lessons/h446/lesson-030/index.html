QUESTION 1 OF 4 · LAST LESSON · L029 · K-MAPS

Retrieval

A Karnaugh map contains four adjacent 1s. How many variables are eliminated by grouping all four?

MODEL ANSWER

Two variables are eliminated. A group of four removes two changing variables; retain only those that remain constant.

QUESTION 2 OF 4 · EARLIER · SIGN AND MAGNITUDE

Retrieval

Represent −13 using 8-bit sign and magnitude.

MODEL ANSWER

Step 1: 13 in binary is 1101. Step 2: pad the magnitude to seven bits: 0001101. Step 3: because the number is negative, put 1 in the sign bit. Answer: 1 | 0001101 = 10001101. This is sign and magnitude, not two’s complement.

QUESTION 3 OF 4 · EARLIER · BINARY SEARCH TREE

Retrieval

Insert 40, 20, 60, 10, 30, 50 into an empty binary search tree. Draw the resulting tree.

MODEL ANSWER

Insert the values in the order given. 40 becomes the root. 20 goes left of 40; 60 goes right of 40. 10 goes left of 20; 30 goes right of 20; 50 goes left of 60. Every smaller value goes to the left and every larger value to the right.

QUESTION 4 OF 4 · CHALLENGE · FLOATING-POINT SUBTRACTION

Challenge

Using a 5-bit two’s complement mantissa and 4-bit two’s complement exponent, calculate A − B. Show exponent alignment and mantissa subtraction. A = 0.1100₂ × 2³; B = 0.1000₂ × 2².

MODEL ANSWER

Align exponents: B = 0.0100₂ × 2³. Subtract mantissas: 0.1100₂ − 0.0100₂ = 0.1000₂. Answer: 0.1000₂ × 2³ = 4.

Start with binary addition

One binary column can produce a sum bit and a carry bit.

0 + 1 = 1
1 + 0 = 1
1 + 1 = 10₂

For 1 + 1, the right-hand bit is Sum = 0; the left-hand bit is Carry = 1.

Find the limitation

The half adder only accepts A and B. It has no third input for Carry-in (Cᵢₙ).
