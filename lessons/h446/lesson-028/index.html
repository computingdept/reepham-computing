QUESTION 1 OF 4

TWO’S COMPLEMENT

Write −13 as an 8-bit two’s-complement integer.

ANSWER

11110011.

Start with +13 = 00001101. Invert the bits → 11110010, then add 1 → 11110011.

QUESTION 2 OF 4

DE MORGAN

For the circuit shown, simplify Q = ¬(A ∨ B) using De Morgan’s Law.

ANSWER

Q = ¬A ∧ ¬B.

NOT of an OR becomes AND of the individually negated inputs. Both expressions are equivalent.

QUESTION 3 OF 4

BUBBLE SORT

Complete one full left-to-right pass of ascending bubble sort. What is the resulting array?

ANSWER

[4, 6, 2, 8, 9].

Compare neighbouring items and swap where needed: [4,9,6,2,8] → [4,6,9,2,8] → [4,6,2,9,8] → [4,6,2,8,9].

QUESTION 4 OF 4

CHALLENGE · NORMALISATION

Normalise this positive binary mantissa without changing its value. Give the new exponent.

ANSWER

0.110100 × 2².

Shift the mantissa point two places right (0.001101 → 0.110100), so decrease the exponent from 4 to 2. The numerical value stays the same.

3 of 6
STEP 4 OF 6

Keep only what stays the same

Look at each input variable across the grouped cells (where Q = 1):

Input stays 1 → write the variable, e.g. A
Input stays 0 → write its NOT, e.g. ¬A, because ¬0 = 1
Input changes 0 ↔ 1 → leave that variable out

In our group, A = 1 in both cells, while B changes. Keep A, leave out B: Q = A.

4 of 6
STEP 5 OF 6

Why Gray code?

Ordinary binary: 00 → 01 → 10 → 11

From 01 to 10, two bits change — these cannot be neighbouring K-map columns.

Gray-code order: 00 → 01 → 11 → 10

Each neighbouring pair differs by one bit only, including the end pair 10 ↔ 00.

5 of 6
STEP 6 OF 6

Read the three-input layout

Two bits label each BC column. Moving sideways changes one input only. This allows useful groups in three-variable maps.

DEVELOP · GRAY CODE

4. Explain Gray-code order

Complete the two missing column labels, then explain why 01 and 11 are adjacent and why the order 00, 01, 10, 11 is unsuitable for a K-map.

Ordinary binary order: 00, 01, 10, 11
K-map Gray-code columns BC: 00, __, 11, __
Compare neighbouring columns: 01 → 11
Think about how many input bits change between neighbouring columns.
