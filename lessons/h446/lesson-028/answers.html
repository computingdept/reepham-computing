Original question · 1 of 7CORE · 2 VARIABLES

1. One horizontal pair

Circle both adjacent 1s. For A and B, state which stays constant or changes. Write the simplified Q.

Worked solution
FINAL ANSWER
Q = ¬A
Explain and self-mark

Circle: Both 1s in the top row make one pair.

Compare: A=0 in both cells. B changes from 0 to 1.

Simplify: A stays 0, so use NOT A (¬A). B changes, so remove B.

Check: Q is 1 whenever A is 0.

Original question · 2 of 7CORE · 2 VARIABLES

2. Just one 1

Which input values produce the only 1? Write Q as one AND term using A and B.

Worked solution
FINAL ANSWER
Q = A ∧ B
Explain and self-mark

Circle: A single 1 at A=1, B=1 cannot join another output 1. A group of one is valid.

Compare: A and B are both fixed at 1.

Simplify: Keep both positive terms with AND.

Check: Q=1 only for A=1 and B=1.

Original question · 3 of 7DEVELOP · 2 VARIABLES

3. Two pairs with an overlap

Circle two useful pairs, identify the fixed input in each, then combine the terms with OR.

Worked solution
FINAL ANSWER
Q = ¬A ∨ B
Explain and self-mark

Group 1: The top row has A=0 throughout; B changes. The term is ¬A.

Group 2: The right column has B=1 throughout; A changes. The term is B.

Overlap: The top-right 1 belongs to both groups; this is allowed.

Simplify: Combine the two terms with OR, giving ¬A ∨ B.

Original question · 4 of 7DEVELOP · GRAY CODE

4. Explain Gray-code order

Complete the two missing column labels, then explain why 01 and 11 are adjacent and why the order 00, 01, 10, 11 is unsuitable for a K-map.

Ordinary binary order: 00, 01, 10, 11
K-map Gray-code columns BC: 00, __, 11, __
Compare neighbouring columns: 01 → 11
Worked solution
FINAL ANSWER
00 → 01 → 11 → 10

Ordinary binary: 00 → 01 → 10 → 11

Gray code: 00 → 01 → 11 → 10

Each neighbouring Gray-code label differs in exactly one bit.

Explain and self-mark

Complete the labels: 00, 01, 11, 10.

Why 01 and 11 are neighbours: Only the first input bit changes (0 → 1); the second bit stays 1.

Why ordinary binary order is unsuitable: 01 → 10 changes both input bits, so those columns are not adjacent in a K-map.

Edge connection: 10 ↔ 00 also changes only one bit; the left and right edges touch conceptually.

Original question · 5 of 7APPLY · 3 VARIABLES

5. Simplify a group of four

The columns use BC Gray-code order. Circle all four 1s, compare A/B/C, then write Q.

Worked solution
FINAL ANSWER
Q = C
Explain and self-mark

Circle: All four 1s form a 2×2 group across BC=01 and BC=11 and both A rows.

Compare: A changes 0 ↔ 1; B changes 0 ↔ 1; C remains 1.

Simplify: Remove A and B. Keep C.

Check: Q=1 whenever C=1, whatever A and B.

Original question · 6 of 7APPLY · 3 VARIABLES

6. Simplify a pair

Circle the two adjacent 1s. Which two inputs stay constant? Write the simplified Boolean term.

Worked solution
FINAL ANSWER
Q = A ∧ C
Explain and self-mark

Circle: The two 1s are in row A=1, under BC=01 and BC=11.

Compare: A stays 1; C stays 1; B changes 0 → 1.

Simplify: Keep A and C joined by AND. Remove B.

Original question · 7 of 7CHALLENGE · ADAPTED OCR CONTEXT

7. Free-popcorn voucher

E = voucher shown, F = soft drink, G = water. Free popcorn is offered when E AND (F OR G). Circle two useful pairs and write R as an OR of two AND terms. Adapted from OCR Boolean Algebra Q2(b).

Worked solution
FINAL ANSWER
R = (E ∧ F) ∨ (E ∧ G)
Explain and self-mark

Group 1: In row E=1, columns FG=01 and 11 give E ∧ G. F changes while E and G stay 1.

Group 2: In row E=1, columns FG=11 and 10 give E ∧ F. G changes while E and F stay 1.

Overlap: FG=11 is in both pairs and that is allowed.

Simplify: Join the two AND terms with OR. An equivalent factored form is E ∧ (F ∨ G).
