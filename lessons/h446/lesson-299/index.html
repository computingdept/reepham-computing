NEW LEARNING · WORKED EXAMPLE FIRST

Trace and predict

// Synthetic records: minutes revised, score.
(10,40), (20,45), (30,65), (40,60), (50,80)
// Identify an association, but do not infer causation.
// Visual: scatter plot, x=revision minutes,
// y=assessment percentage.
STEP 1 OF 3

Identify the question

Data mining searches for useful patterns in a set of records; decide what question the analysis should answer.

STEP 2 OF 3

Choose a suitable chart

Scatter charts show relationships between two numeric variables; bars compare categories; histograms show numeric distributions.

STEP 3 OF 3

Interpret responsibly

An association does not prove that one variable causes another. Consider sample size, bias, missing data and outliers.

I DO · WORKED MODEL

For observations (1,2), (2,4), (3,6), describe the association without claiming causation.

x: 1 2 3
y: 2 4 6
Positive linear association in sample.
STEP 1 OF 2

Predict first

As x increases, y increases in this small sample, indicating positive association.

STEP 2 OF 2

Explain the method

No causal claim is justified without additional evidence or experimental design.

WE DO · DIFFERENT PROBLEM

For scores [40,42,45,90], select a suitable visual and explain a limitation of the mean.

Mean=(40+42+45+90)/4=54.25
Median=(42+45)/2=43.5
STEP 1 OF 2

Predict first

A box plot or ordered dot plot shows the unusually high score 90 clearly.

STEP 2 OF 2

Explain the method

The mean 54.25 is pulled upwards by the outlier; median 43.5 better describes the centre of the lower cluster.

Why can an outlier distort the mean?

EXAM QUESTION 1 OF 4 · 3 MARKS · 3 MINUTES

A large cinema-booking website stores millions of visits, searches and ticket purchases. Describe what data mining means when analysing its records. [3]

OCR H446/02 · June 2025 · Q2(b)(i) · RHS exam-style scenario

MODEL ANSWER · 3 MARKS

Data mining analyses large volumes of data, derives useful information from them, and identifies patterns, trends, anomalies or relationships not immediately obvious.

Marking: Three separate marks for large-scale analysis; making data useful; identifying hidden patterns/trends/relationships/anomalies.

EXAM QUESTION 2 OF 4 · 2 MARKS · 2 MINUTES

The cinema website notices patterns in customers' searches and purchases. Explain one way it could use a discovered pattern to improve the content or functionality it shows visitors. [2]

OCR H446/02 · June 2025 · Q2(b)(ii) · RHS exam-style scenario

MODEL ANSWER · 2 MARKS

For example, find that families buying weekend tickets often search for a particular film genre; then show appropriate upcoming family films or tailored adverts to that visitor group.

Marking: One mark for identifying a relevant mined pattern; one for a linked and credible improvement/personalisation to the site.

EXAM QUESTION 3 OF 4 · 9 MARKS · 9 MINUTES

A multiplayer history game logs how thousands of visitors explore rooms, buy digital artefacts and complete missions. The developers want to apply DATA MINING before releasing another version. Discuss how patterns and anomalies might guide design and business decisions, the limitations of correlation and incomplete datasets, and the ethical risks of analysing users' behaviour. Evaluate whether the investment is worthwhile. [9]

OCR H446/02 · November 2021 · Q1(e)* · RHS exam-style scenario

MODEL ANSWER · 9 MARKS

Data mining searches large sets to discover nonobvious patterns, links and outliers. Group dwell times, repeated early exits, purchases and seldom-visited rooms can guide new content, navigation and difficulty; potential revenue changes may follow. Risks include privacy, data security, bias, misleading causal assumptions and resource cost. Propose responsible data minimisation and testing with stakeholder feedback, then justify investment.

Marking: Nine-mark holistic teacher assessment: detailed balanced contextual evaluation 7–9; sound 4–6; basic 1–3; none zero.

RHS ADDITIONAL PRACTICE · QUESTION 4 OF 4 · 2 MARKS · 2 MINUTES

A supermarket wants to compare monthly sales of four product categories over one year. Suggest a suitable visualisation for showing changes over time and explain your choice. [2]

RHS original follow-on question · linked to this lesson's exam practice

MODEL ANSWER · 2 MARKS

A line chart with a separate line for each category shows trends over time and allows changes in sales to be compared.

Marking: One mark for a suitable chart; one for explaining how it reveals changes or comparisons over time.
