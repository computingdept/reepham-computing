NEW LEARNING · WORKED EXAMPLE FIRST

Trace and predict

// Synthetic records: minutes revised, score.
(10,40), (20,45), (30,65), (40,60), (50,80)
// Identify an association, but do not infer causation.
// Visual: scatter plot, x=revision minutes,
// y=assessment percentage.
STEP 1 OF 3

Identify the question

Data mining searches for useful patterns in a set of records; decide what question the analysis should answer.

STEP 2 OF 3

Choose a suitable chart

Scatter charts show relationships between two numeric variables; bars compare categories; histograms show numeric distributions.

STEP 3 OF 3

Interpret responsibly

An association does not prove that one variable causes another. Consider sample size, bias, missing data and outliers.

I DO · WORKED MODEL

For observations (1,2), (2,4), (3,6), describe the association without claiming causation.

x: 1 2 3
y: 2 4 6
Positive linear association in sample.
STEP 1 OF 2

Predict first

As x increases, y increases in this small sample, indicating positive association.

STEP 2 OF 2

Explain the method

No causal claim is justified without additional evidence or experimental design.

WE DO · DIFFERENT PROBLEM

For scores [40,42,45,90], select a suitable visual and explain a limitation of the mean.

Mean=(40+42+45+90)/4=54.25
Median=(42+45)/2=43.5
STEP 1 OF 2

Predict first

A box plot or ordered dot plot shows the unusually high score 90 clearly.

STEP 2 OF 2

Explain the method

The mean 54.25 is pulled upwards by the outlier; median 43.5 better describes the centre of the lower cluster.
