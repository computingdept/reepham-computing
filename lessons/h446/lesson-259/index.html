Magnetic, optical and flash storage

By the end, you can…
  • Explain how magnetic, optical and flash storage work
  • Compare access and physical durability
  • Justify one medium for a practical use

Key vocabulary

magneticopticalflashseek latencybackup
NEW LEARNING · EXAMPLE FIRST

Trace the concept

HDD → magnetic regions on rotating platters.
DVD → optical disc read by laser.
SSD / USB flash → non-volatile cells.
Choose by capacity, cost, speed and context.
STEP 1 OF 3

Identify the storage mechanism

HDDs use magnetised regions on rotating platters; DVD-style media are read optically.

STEP 2 OF 3

Explain solid-state storage

Flash cells are non-volatile electronic storage without rotating platters or mechanical head seeks.

STEP 3 OF 3

Evaluate rather than rank universally

SSD generally has low seek latency and resists mechanical shock; HDD may offer lower cost per GB at large capacities.

I DO · MODEL

Choose SSD or HDD for a photographer moving large images between sites.

High portability; frequent access.
SSD: low seek latency, no moving head.
HDD: lower cost per GB often.
STEP 1 OF 2

Predict

An SSD is attractive for frequent portable access and avoiding moving parts.

STEP 2 OF 2

Explain

An HDD can offer capacity at a lower cost; backups remain necessary regardless.

WE DO · DIFFERENT PROBLEM

A school distributes a fixed offline exhibition to computers with disc drives. Would an optical disc work?

Static media, compatible optical drives.
Read-only distribution is possible.
STEP 1 OF 2

Predict

An optical disc can distribute static content without needing a network connection.

STEP 2 OF 2

Explain

Check disc and drive compatibility and whether updates/reuse are needed.

Foundation · 6 marks · QUESTION 1 OF 3

Give storage technology and one physical feature for HDD, DVD and USB flash.

WORKED ANSWER

[6] HDD magnetic [1] spinning platter [1]; DVD optical [1] laser-read pattern [1]; USB flash semiconductor [1] non-volatile cells [1].

Core · 6 marks · QUESTION 2 OF 3

Explain two benefits and one drawback of an SSD rather than an HDD for frequent portable editing.

WORKED ANSWER

[6] Faster random access [2], shock resistance due to no moving mechanism [2], typically higher cost per GB [2].

Challenge · 6 marks · QUESTION 3 OF 3

Propose fast working storage and bulk archival storage for a school, explaining backup strategy.

WORKED ANSWER

[6] SSD for regular work/low latency [2]; HDD or suitable archival medium for infrequent bulk data [2]; maintain separate secure backups and check recovery [2].
