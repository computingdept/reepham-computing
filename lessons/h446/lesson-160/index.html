Lesson 160 · Investigatory Powers: RIPA 2000, Modern Safeguards and Ethics

By the end you can…
  • RIPA 2000 sets out specified investigatory powers and safeguards; provisions have changed over time.
  • Investigatory Powers Act 2016 and later changes govern major communications powers and oversight.
  • Intrusion should be assessed against lawful authority, necessity, proportionality and oversight.

OCR 1.5.1 identifies RIPA 2000. This lesson also distinguishes later investigatory-powers legislation; ethical questions introduce but do not exhaust OCR 1.5.2.

I DO · MODELLED APPLICATION

A public authority requests covert interception of communications to investigate a serious threat. Is 'for public safety' alone an adequate legal justification?

STEP 1 OF 3

Identify proposed interception

Identify the relevant facts, purpose and authority.

STEP 2 OF 3

Check lawful powers

Discuss which law or safeguard fits the situation.

STEP 3 OF 3

Authorisation + oversight

No. Relevant statutory powers, lawful authorisation, necessity, proportionality and appropriate safeguards/oversight must be considered. Exact procedures depend on activity and legal power. OCR names RIPA 2000, while IPA 2016 now covers major investigatory communications powers.

WE DO · DIFFERENT CASE

A school wants to monitor activity on managed computers to investigate misuse. Is this identical to state interception under RIPA?

STEP 1 OF 3

Managed school devices

Identify the relevant facts, purpose and authority.

STEP 2 OF 3

Privacy and safeguarding

Discuss which law or safeguard fits the situation.

STEP 3 OF 3

Apply proportionate policy

No. School monitoring has its own employment, safeguarding, privacy and policy considerations and must be lawful, transparent and proportionate as appropriate. Do not claim all ordinary IT logging requires a state-interception warrant.
