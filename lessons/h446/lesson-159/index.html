Lesson 159 · Copyright, Designs and Patents Act 1988: Code and Digital Content

By the end you can…
  • Original software code, images, text and music can attract copyright automatically.
  • Licence terms, ownership and limited exceptions determine permitted copying and reuse.
  • Respect allowed distribution, modification, attribution and restrictions.

The CDPA 1988 remains relevant, as amended. Educational and fair-dealing exceptions are limited; public posting is not automatically permitted.

I DO · MODELLED APPLICATION

A pupil copies an online graphic into a public school website, includes the photographer's name but has no permission. Is attribution enough?

STEP 1 OF 3

Protected image

Identify the relevant facts, purpose and authority.

STEP 2 OF 3

Attribution only

Discuss which law or safeguard fits the situation.

STEP 3 OF 3

Check licence/permission

Not generally. Crediting the creator is good practice but does not itself grant permission. Check the licence or applicable limited exception before publicly reproducing the image; choose licensed/own work if uncertain.

WE DO · DIFFERENT CASE

A developer discovers code labelled MIT Licence and wants to reuse it in a new app. What must they check?

STEP 1 OF 3

Code with licence

Identify the relevant facts, purpose and authority.

STEP 2 OF 3

Read conditions

Discuss which law or safeguard fits the situation.

STEP 3 OF 3

Retain notices as required

A permissive licence may allow use/modification/distribution subject to its terms, generally keeping the copyright/licence notice. Check the exact code's licence and third-party dependencies rather than assuming all free online code is unrestricted.

True or false?

Any copyrighted image can be republished legally if the creator's name is shown.

Model answer

False. Attribution alone does not confer copyright permission.
