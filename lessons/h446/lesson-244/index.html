Thinking concurrently: independent tasks

By the end, you can…
  • Identify which tasks can happen at the same time
  • Model dependencies between operations
  • Calculate benefits and overheads of concurrent execution

Vocabulary

concurrencydependencycritical pathrace conditionsynchronisation
NEW LEARNING · CODE FIRST

Predict and explain

// Report production tasks:
// Download dataset  (4 min)
// Resize images     (3 min)
// Assemble report    (2 min; needs both inputs)
// Sequential: 4+3+2 = 9 min
// Parallel: max(4,3)+2 = 6 min
STEP 1 OF 3

Find tasks without dependencies

Dataset download and image processing can operate concurrently if they use separate resources.

STEP 2 OF 3

Respect the final dependency

Report assembly cannot start until BOTH preparation tasks finish.

STEP 3 OF 3

Calculate the ideal gain

Sequential 9 minutes, parallel 6 minutes. Actual performance depends on scheduling and resource contention.

I DO · MODEL

Two independent data checks take 5 and 3 minutes; publishing takes 2 more. Calculate ideal sequential and concurrent times.

Sequential = 5+3+2 = 10
Concurrent = max(5,3)+2 = 7
STEP 1 OF 2

Predict the method

Add all durations for the sequential path: 10 minutes.

STEP 2 OF 2

Check and explain

Run independent checks together, wait 5 minutes, then publish in 2: total 7 minutes.

WE DO · DIFFERENT PROBLEM

Images take 4 minutes and CSS 6; deployment needs 1 after both. Compare approaches.

Sequential=4+6+1=11
Concurrent=max(4,6)+1=7
STEP 1 OF 2

Predict the method

Images and CSS are independent preparation tasks; calculate 4+6+1=11 minutes sequentially.

STEP 2 OF 2

Check and explain

Run both together; CSS sets the critical 6-minute time. Then deploy 1 minute: 7 minutes.

Foundation · 4 marks · QUESTION 1 OF 3

Tasks A (3 min) and B (5 min) are independent, then final C (2 min). Find sequential and ideal parallel completion times.

FULL MODEL ANSWER

[4] Sequential 3+5+2=10 minutes [2]. Parallel max(3,5)+2=7 minutes [2].

Core · 6 marks · QUESTION 2 OF 3

Describe why logging and updating a shared bank balance cannot be safely performed as two uncoordinated write operations. Propose a safeguard.

FULL MODEL ANSWER

[6] Both operations may read the same old balance [1], overwrite each other's changes [2]; lock/transaction ensures one update is committed safely [2]; some concurrency advantage may be lost due to waiting [1].

Stretch · 6 marks · QUESTION 3 OF 3

Four tasks: parse A=4 min, fetch B=6 min, validate C=2 min (requires A and B), publish D=1 min (requires C). Find shortest ideal time and explain two constraints.

FULL MODEL ANSWER

[6] A and B overlap [1]; complete after max(4,6)=6 [1]; C then 2 gives 8 [1]; D then 1 gives 9 minutes [1]; overhead [1] and limited shared resources/communication [1].

TERM 1 OF 5

concurrency

Tasks making progress over overlapping intervals.

TERM 2 OF 5

dependency

A task needs another result first.

TERM 3 OF 5

critical path

Longest dependent chain determining ideal completion time.

TERM 4 OF 5

race condition

Timing-dependent outcome caused by unsafe shared access.

TERM 5 OF 5

synchronisation

Coordination of operations using shared state.

True or false?

All parts of an algorithm can always run concurrently if enough cores are available.

Model answer

False. Dependencies and shared-state constraints may require sequential execution.

Confidence check
