QUESTION 4 OF 4 · YEAR 12 CHALLENGE · LESSON 003 · SHIFTS & MASKS

Year 12 challenge

Apply an eight-bit left shift by two to 00010110₂ and bitwise AND to 00110110₂ with 00001111₂. Give both results in binary.

MODEL ANSWER

Left shift 00010110 << 2 = 01011000 (eight-bit, zero-filled). AND 00110110 ∧ 00001111 = 00000110.

STEP 1 OF 3

Analyse the loops

One full loop runs n iterations; a pair of n-sized nested loops performs n² inner iterations.

STEP 2 OF 3

Separate time and space

Big O time counts growth of work; auxiliary space measures extra storage beyond the original input.

STEP 3 OF 3

Compare feasible scales

At n=16, exponential growth can be dramatically more expensive than linear growth. Big O omits constants and measured run time.

I DO · MODEL

For n=8, compare one loop visiting each item with two full nested loops.

int n=8;
for(int i=0;i<n;i++) Work(); // 8
for(int i=0;i<n;i++)
 for(int j=0;j<n;j++) Work(); // 64
STEP 1 OF 2

Predict first

One scan has eight iterations, giving linear growth O(n).

STEP 2 OF 2

Explain the answer

Nested loops perform 8×8=64 inner iterations, giving quadratic growth O(n²).

WE DO · DIFFERENT PROBLEM

For n=16, compare linear and quadratic operation counts. What happens when n doubles from 8?

linear = n; quadratic = n*n;
// For n=16: 16 and 256
STEP 1 OF 2

Predict first

The linear count doubles from 8 to 16.

STEP 2 OF 2

Explain the answer

Quadratic count rises from 64 to 256, multiplying by four; this difference grows with n.

WORKED ANSWER

[6] Merge sort can scale better to large n [2]; O(n log n) vs O(n²) [1]; in-place sort can suit small data or tight memory [1]; typically O(1) auxiliary space for in-place quadratic sorting [1]; compare time-space trade-off [1].
