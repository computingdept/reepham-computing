QUESTION 1 OF 4

Recall

Where is the implied binary point in a two’s-complement floating-point mantissa?

ANSWER

Immediately after the sign bit.

The mantissa is treated as a signed binary fraction. The first bit carries the negative place value; the remaining bits represent fractional place values.

QUESTION 2 OF 4

Apply

Write 0.625 as a binary fraction. Show the fractional place values.

ANSWER

0.101₂

½ + ⅛ = 0.5 + 0.125 = 0.625. The ¼ place is 0, so the fractional bits are 101.

QUESTION 3 OF 4

Represent

Encode 6.5 in normalised eight-bit mantissa / four-bit exponent format.

ANSWER

01101000 0011

6.5 = 110.1₂. Normalise to 0.1101₂ × 2³. The mantissa is therefore 01101000 and the exponent is 0011.

QUESTION 4 OF 4

Reason

Normalise 00011010 0101 in 8+4 format. Explain why the exponent changes.

ANSWER

01101000 0011

Move the mantissa left twice so it begins 01. Each left shift multiplies the mantissa by 2, so two shifts multiply it by 4. Reduce the exponent by 2 to keep the overall value unchanged: 0101 → 0011.

LESSON 005 · OCR H446 1.4.1(g)1 minute

Floating point → denary

By the end, you can…
  • evaluate the signed mantissa and exponent separately;
  • decode positive and negative values with positive or negative exponents;
  • recognise normalisation and explain precision versus range.
Key vocabulary for today
mantissa exponent normalised precision range two’s complement

Use an eight-bit mantissa and four-bit exponent unless a question states otherwise. Both fields are two’s complement; the mantissa’s point follows the sign bit.

NEW LEARNING6 minutes

Split → evaluate → scale

8-bit mantissa: −1 | ½  ¼  ⅛  ¹⁄₁₆  ¹⁄₃₂  ¹⁄₆₄  ¹⁄₁₂₈
4-bit exponent: −8   4   2   1

Value = mantissa × 2ᴱ
Two signs, two jobs:
the mantissa controls the sign of the value; the exponent controls its scale.
STEP 1 OF 5

Split the fields

Separate the bit pattern into the mantissa and the exponent at the stated field boundary.

Treat the two fields separately before doing any calculation.
1 of 5
STEP 2 OF 5

Evaluate the mantissa

Add the place values for the bits that are 1.

Mantissa places:
−1 | ½  ¼  ⅛  ¹⁄₁₆  ¹⁄₃₂  ¹⁄₆₄  ¹⁄₁₂₈
If the sign bit is 1, include the −1 place value.
2 of 5
STEP 3 OF 5

Decode the exponent

Read the exponent as a signed two’s-complement integer.

4-bit exponent places:
−8   4   2   1
A negative exponent changes the scale. It does not make the value negative.
3 of 5
STEP 4 OF 5

Apply the scale

Value = mantissa × 2ᴱ

A positive exponent multiplies by a power of two. A negative exponent divides by a power of two.

Example: 2⁻² = ¼.
4 of 5
STEP 5 OF 5

Check the representation

For a non-zero normalised mantissa:

  • 01 starts a positive normalised mantissa.
  • 10 starts a negative normalised mantissa.
More mantissa bits improve precision. More exponent bits increase range.
5 of 5

KEY VOCABULARYRetrieval check
QUESTION 1 OF 6

Recall the term

Which part of a floating-point number stores the signed fractional value?

1 of 6
ANSWER
Mantissa

The mantissa stores the signed significant part of the floating-point value.

QUESTION 2 OF 6

Recall the term

Which part controls the scale of a floating-point value?

2 of 6
ANSWER
Exponent

The exponent supplies the power of two used to scale the mantissa.

QUESTION 3 OF 6

Recall the term

What word describes a non-zero mantissa arranged to begin 01 if positive or 10 if negative?

3 of 6
ANSWER
Normalised

Normalisation uses the available mantissa bits efficiently without changing the represented value.

QUESTION 4 OF 6

Complete the statement

More mantissa bits improve the ________ of a floating-point representation.

4 of 6
ANSWER
Precision

More mantissa bits allow values to be represented more precisely.

QUESTION 5 OF 6

Complete the statement

More exponent bits increase the ________ of values that can be represented.

5 of 6
ANSWER
Range

A wider exponent allows much larger and much smaller magnitudes to be represented.

QUESTION 6 OF 6

Recall the representation

What signed binary representation gives the most significant bit a negative place value?

6 of 6
ANSWER
Two’s complement

In this lesson, both the mantissa and exponent are interpreted using two’s complement.

The second mantissa begins 11, so it is not normalised. Exponent 1111 means scale by 2⁻¹; the mantissa determines the sign.
