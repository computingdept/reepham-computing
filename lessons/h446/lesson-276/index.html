By the end you can…
  • ERDs model entities and relationship cardinalities before implementing tables.
  • One-to-many relationships are typically represented with a foreign key on the many side.
  • Many-to-many relationships require an associative/junction table when translated into a relational schema, often with two foreign keys.

AUTHOR 1 → MANY BOOK
Book.AuthorID FK
STEP 1 OF 2

Predict first

Each Book row stores one AuthorID

STEP 2 OF 2

Model answer

several books can refer to the same author.

WE DO · DIFFERENT EXAMPLE

Model a pupil taking several courses and each course having several pupils.

Pupil MANY ↔ MANY Course
Enrolment(PupilID FK,CourseID FK)
STEP 1 OF 2

Predict first

Create Enrolment junction table

STEP 2 OF 2

Model answer

each enrolment references exactly one pupil and course.

QUESTION 1 OF 3

State meanings of 1:1,1:M and M:N cardinalities. [3]

WORKED ANSWER

Exactly one to one [1], one related to many [1], many to many [1].

QUESTION 2 OF 3

Draw/describe an ERD for Customer and Order with one customer placing many orders. Give foreign key. [6]

WORKED ANSWER

Customer 1→many Order [2]; Customer.CustomerID PK [1]; Order.OrderID PK [1]; Order.CustomerID FK [2].

QUESTION 3 OF 3

Resolve Student many-to-many Course into relational tables; describe keys and integrity. [6]

WORKED ANSWER

Student(PupilID PK) [1], Course(CourseID PK) [1], Enrolment junction [1] with PupilID and CourseID FKs [2], referential integrity prevents orphan enrolments [1].
