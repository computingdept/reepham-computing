AI, automated decisions, workforce and privacy

By the end you can…
  • Automated decision-making can increase consistency and speed, but outputs depend on data quality, modelling choices and deployment conditions.
  • AI systems can embed historical bias, use proxies for protected characteristics, make errors or generate opaque recommendations. Real-world consequences require human oversight and appropriate evaluation.
  • Workforce change can automate repetitive tasks while creating new roles. Privacy, proportionality, consent/lawful basis, transparency and the right to challenge decisions matter.

I DO · MODEL

A hiring algorithm disproportionately rejects candidates with career breaks. Identify a risk and mitigation.

Risk: training-data proxy bias.
Mitigation: audit outcomes + features;
human review + meaningful appeals.
STEP 1 OF 2

Predict first

Career breaks may correlate with personal circumstances; scoring can embed discriminatory proxy effects.

STEP 2 OF 2

Explain

Audit outcomes across groups, minimise unnecessary features, provide explanation and allow human challenge.

WE DO · DIFFERENT PROBLEM

A school predicts exam outcomes using incomplete attendance data. What should happen before acting?

Check data quality → verify context
→ review with teacher/pupil → offer support.
STEP 1 OF 2

Predict first

Missing or late attendance entries may make the model score unreliable or misleading.

STEP 2 OF 2

Explain

Human checking and supportive proportionate action are safer than automatic punitive intervention.
