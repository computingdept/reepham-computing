NEW LEARNING · 1 OF 6

Why do we need flip-flops?

Logic gates calculate; a flip-flop remembers. Registers must hold values even when other inputs change.

NEW LEARNING · 2 OF 6

Meet the D-type flip-flop

D is the data input. Q is the stored output. CLK determines when Q can update.

NEW LEARNING · 3 OF 6

The rising edge matters

In this lesson we use a rising-edge-triggered D flip-flop: Q copies D only when CLK changes from 0 to 1.

NEW LEARNING · 4 OF 6

Between clock edges, Q holds

Changing D alone does not change Q. Without a rising edge, the flip-flop keeps its previous bit.

NEW LEARNING · 5 OF 6

Trace the timing diagram

At rising edges 1, 2 and 3, read D. Q updates to 1, 0 and 1 respectively and stays constant between edges.

NEW LEARNING · 6 OF 6

Build a register

Eight D-type flip-flops, sharing a clock, can store eight bits (one byte) simultaneously.
