QUESTION 1 OF 4 · LAST LESSON · L030 · HALF ADDERS

Retrieval

A half adder has A = 1 and B = 1. State its Sum and Carry outputs.

MODEL ANSWER

Sum = 0 (1 XOR 1). Carry = 1 (1 AND 1). Together they represent binary 10.

QUESTION 2 OF 4 · EARLIER LEARNING · BOOLEAN ALGEBRA

Retrieval

Simplify ¬(A ∧ B) using De Morgan’s law.

MODEL ANSWER

¬A ∨ ¬B. Negate each input and change AND to OR.

QUESTION 3 OF 4 · EARLIER LEARNING · BINARY SEARCH TREE

Retrieval

Insert 12, 5, 18, 9 into an empty binary search tree. Where is 9 positioned?

MODEL ANSWER

9 is the right child of 5. Root 12; 5 is left of 12; 18 is right of 12; 9 is right of 5.

QUESTION 4 OF 4 · CHALLENGE · FULL ADDER

Challenge

A full adder receives A = 1, B = 1 and CarryIn = 1. Work out Sum and CarryOut, showing the binary total.

MODEL ANSWER

1 + 1 + 1 = 3 = 11₂. Sum = 1 (least-significant bit), CarryOut = 1 (higher bit).

NEW LEARNING · 1 OF 6

Why do we need flip-flops?

Logic gates calculate; a flip-flop remembers. Registers must hold values even when other inputs change.

NEW LEARNING · 2 OF 6

Meet the D-type flip-flop

D is the data input. Q is the stored output. CLK determines when Q can update.

NEW LEARNING · 3 OF 6

The rising edge matters

In this lesson we use a rising-edge-triggered D flip-flop: Q copies D only when CLK changes from 0 to 1.

NEW LEARNING · 4 OF 6

Between clock edges, Q holds

Changing D alone does not change Q. Without a rising edge, the flip-flop keeps its previous bit.
