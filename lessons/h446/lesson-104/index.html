Lesson 104 · Magnetic, Optical and Flash Storage

By the end you can…
  • Magnetic hard disks use magnetised regions on spinning platters with moving heads; they can offer large capacity at relatively low cost per GB.
  • Optical discs are read with a laser, often used for physically distributed or archived/removable media, though capacity and drive availability vary.
  • Flash uses electronic non-volatile memory cells without moving read/write parts; often faster random access and more robust to vibration than HDDs, but trade-offs remain.

I DO · MODEL

Choose portable storage for a field photographer travelling over rough ground with active projects.

WORKED REASONING

Explain the choice

Flash SSD: no spinning platter and generally quick random access; still requires backups.

WE DO · DIFFERENT EXAMPLE

A school wants inexpensive bulk storage for a large local video archive, with separate backups.

WORKED REASONING

Explain the choice

Magnetic HDD is a reasonable candidate because of capacity and cost per GB; validate resilience and transfer needs.

APPLICATION · QUESTION 2 OF 3

Explain one advantage and one limitation of flash SSD over magnetic HDD for a mobile video editor. [6]

MARK SCHEME / WORKED ANSWER

No moving parts → vibration resistance [2], fast random access [2], often higher cost/GB or write endurance trade-off [2].
