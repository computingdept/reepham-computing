I DO · WORKED MODEL

A 3-packet file crosses a packet-switched network. Packets arrive 2,1,3. Explain handling.

STEP 1 OF 3

Send packets 1,2,3

Identify the relevant information and make a prediction.

STEP 2 OF 3

Receive 2,1,3

Explain the change using correct technical terms.

STEP 3 OF 3

Reorder 1,2,3

Packets can follow different routes or encounter different queue delays. With a reliable transport such as TCP, sequence numbers permit correct reordering before delivery of the byte stream; missing data can be retransmitted.

WE DO · DIFFERENT PROBLEM

A telephone system reserves capacity for a call before any voice data flows. Compare to ordinary IP networking.

STEP 1 OF 3

Reserve dedicated circuit

Identify the relevant information and make a prediction.

STEP 2 OF 3

Hold resources during call

Explain the change using correct technical terms.

STEP 3 OF 3

Compare shared packet links

A circuit-switched system sets aside end-to-end resources while active; ordinary packet-switched IP shares links dynamically with others. Circuit switching offers predictable reserved capacity but can waste it during silence; packet switching adapts.

TERM 1/4

packet switching

Data divided into packets that may take different network routes.

TERM 2/4

circuit switching

A dedicated end-to-end circuit established before communication.

TERM 3/4

latency

Delay experienced by data in transit.

TERM 4/4

retransmission

Sending data again when loss is detected by a reliable protocol.

True or false?

IP alone guarantees that every packet is received in the original order.

Model answer

False. IP offers best-effort delivery; transport protocols may provide ordering and retransmission.
