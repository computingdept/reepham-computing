Predict the effect

using System;
int n=4;
int linearChecks=0;
for(int i=0;i<n;i++) linearChecks++;
int quadraticChecks=0;
for(int i=0;i<n;i++)
  for(int j=0;j<n;j++) quadraticChecks++;
Console.WriteLine($"{linearChecks}, {quadraticChecks}"); // 4, 16
STEP 1 OF 3

Start with n

n is the number of items. More items usually mean more operations.

STEP 2 OF 3

Trace loop nesting

One full scan performs n steps. A nested n-by-n scan performs n² steps.

STEP 3 OF 3

Compare growth

When n doubles, linear work doubles; quadratic work roughly quadruples. Big O describes growth, not seconds.

I DO · MODEL

Compare operation counts of one loop and two nested loops for n=5.

Linear: 5 iterations
Nested: 5×5 iterations
STEP 1 OF 2

Predict

One loop visits five elements, so does five operations.

STEP 2 OF 2

Explain

The nested loop repeats five operations five times, making 25.

WE DO · DIFFERENT GUIDED PROBLEM

Compare linear and binary search for 64 ordered values.

Linear: scan candidates.
Binary: discard half each step.
STEP 1 OF 2

Predict

Linear search can check all 64 values.

STEP 2 OF 2

Explain

A standard midpoint binary search needs at most seven comparisons on 64 sorted values.

Foundation · 1 OF 3

Give the Big O of a single full scan of n elements.

MODEL ANSWER

O(n).

Core · 2 OF 3

For n=100, give iterations of a linear n loop and nested n×n loop.

MODEL ANSWER

100 and 10,000 respectively.

Stretch · 3 OF 3

Why may linear search be preferable for a single search of a short unsorted array?

MODEL ANSWER

It does not need the sorting preparation required before binary search.

TERM 1 OF 5

Big O

Asymptotic growth of algorithm work.

TERM 2 OF 5

O(1)

Constant growth.

TERM 3 OF 5

O(log n)

Logarithmic growth, e.g. halving search intervals.

TERM 4 OF 5

O(n)

Linear growth.

TERM 5 OF 5

O(n²)

Quadratic growth, e.g. complete nested scans.

True or false?

O(n²) means an algorithm takes n² seconds.

Model answer

False. It describes growth of work, not a fixed duration.
