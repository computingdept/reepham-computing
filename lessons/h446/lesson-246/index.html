NEW LEARNING · CODE FIRST

Predict and explain

// Vague: 'Make the school timetable fair.'
// Computable part:
// Inputs: lessons, staff, available rooms, slots.
// Rules: one teacher and room per slot.
// Output: clash-free timetable or no solution.
// A scoring criterion must define 'fair'.
STEP 1 OF 3

Recognise ambiguity

'Fair' is subjective until objective criteria are supplied, such as balancing first-period lessons across teachers.

STEP 2 OF 3

Make requirements testable

Represent rooms, lessons and slots; specify hard constraints and a success test.

STEP 3 OF 3

Select techniques appropriately

Decompose into assign/check/evaluate modules. Divide and conquer is useful where subproblems can be solved and combined consistently; not every decomposition is divide and conquer.

I DO · MODEL

Rewrite 'choose the best delivery van route' into a computational task.

Input: locations and journey times
Constraint: visit required stops
Objective: minimise total travel time
STEP 1 OF 2

Predict the method

Replace vague 'best' with a measurable objective such as minimum total travel time.

STEP 2 OF 2

Check and explain

Specify route constraints and verify a produced route covers all required locations.

WE DO · DIFFERENT PROBLEM

A school wants the 'fairest' allocation of laptops across classes. Make it computable.

Inputs: stock, demand per class, priorities
Output: allocations respecting stock
Criterion: minimise unmet essential demand
STEP 1 OF 2

Predict the method

Use explicit demand and stock data and define what fairness means with measurable priorities.

STEP 2 OF 2

Check and explain

Check total allocated never exceeds stock and each class allocation follows the stated policy.

Compare decomposition and divide-and-conquer for sorting 1,000 items, using merge sort as an example.

FULL MODEL ANSWER

[6] Decomposition splits the overall task into subproblems [1]; divide-and-conquer recursively splits input [1], sorts sublists [1] and merges them [1]; combined result is sorted [1], recurrence gives O(n log n) work for merge sort [1].

True or false?

An unclear human judgement becomes computable simply by choosing a faster processor.

Model answer

False. It must first be expressed with measurable inputs, rules and success criteria.
