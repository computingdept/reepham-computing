Dijkstra: shortest paths I

By the end you can…
  • Apply Dijkstra only when edge weights are non-negative
  • Build a tentative-distance table
  • Settle nodes in the correct order and reconstruct the shortest route

Keywords

tentative distancesettledrelaxationpredecessornon-negative weight
CODE / ALGORITHM FIRST

Work through the algorithm

// Dijkstra: conceptual pseudocode
set distance(start)=0; all others=∞
repeat:
  choose unsettled vertex with smallest distance
  mark it settled
  for each neighbour reachable from it:
    candidate=distance(current)+edgeWeight
    if candidate < distance(neighbour):
      update distance and predecessor
// Requires non-negative edge weights.
STEP 1 OF 5

Initialise from the start

Distance(A)=0, all others infinity. Relax A's edges to set B=4 and C=2.

STEP 2 OF 5

Always choose smallest tentative distance

C=2 settles before B=4. Through C, B improves to 3.

STEP 3 OF 5

Keep updating neighbours

Settling B changes D from 10 to 8; settling D changes E from 12 to 10.

STEP 4 OF 5

Reconstruct from predecessors

E←D←B←C←A. Reverse these arrows for A→C→B→D→E, total distance 10.

STEP 5 OF 5

Dijkstra's requirement

Edge weights must be non-negative. A visited/settled node's shortest distance is then final.

I DO · MODEL

Find the shortest P→S route using non-negative weights.

Edges: P-Q=3, P-R=7, Q-R=2, Q-S=6, R-S=1.
STEP 1 OF 4

Predict the next state

P gives tentative Q=3 and R=7.

STEP 2 OF 4

Reveal and explain

Settle Q=3, improving R to 5 and discovering S=9.

STEP 3 OF 4

Reveal and explain

Settle R=5, improving S to 6.

STEP 4 OF 4

Reveal and explain

Predecessors S←R←Q←P give P→Q→R→S, cost 6.

WE DO · DIFFERENT PROBLEM

Find the shortest M→P route using non-negative weights.

Edges: M-N=5, M-O=2, O-N=1, N-P=3, O-P=8.
STEP 1 OF 3

Predict the next state

From M, set N=5 and O=2; O is the next vertex settled.

STEP 2 OF 3

Reveal and explain

From O, N improves from 5 to 3 and P gets a tentative 10.

STEP 3 OF 3

Reveal and explain

From N, P improves to 6; P is next. Route M→O→N→P costs 6.

MODEL ANSWER

[3] A=0 [1], B=4 [1], C=2 [1]. C is chosen next.

Core · 6 marks · QUESTION 2 OF 3

On edges S–A=3, S–B=7, A–B=2, B–T=2 and A–T=8, find the shortest S→T path with full distance reasoning.

MODEL ANSWER

[6] After S: A3, B7 [1]; settle A3 [1] improving B to5 and T to11 [2]; settle B5, T improves to7 [1]; route S→A→B→T, cost 7 [1].

Stretch · 5 marks · QUESTION 3 OF 3

Explain why Dijkstra must not be relied upon when negative-weight edges are possible, and contrast tentative and settled distances.

MODEL ANSWER

[5] Dijkstra assumes non-negative edges [1], so no later path can improve a settled vertex [1]; a negative edge can invalidate that assumption [1]. Tentative distances can change [1], but settled distances are final under the precondition [1].

TERM 1 OF 5

tentative distance

Best known cost to an unsettled vertex.

TERM 2 OF 5

settled

Vertex whose shortest distance has been finalised.

TERM 3 OF 5

relaxation

Improve a neighbour's distance via the current vertex.

TERM 4 OF 5

predecessor

Previous vertex on the best-known route.

TERM 5 OF 5

non-negative weight

Edge cost of zero or greater.

True or false?

Dijkstra always chooses the next vertex by its smallest edge weight.

Model answer

False. It chooses the unsettled vertex with the smallest total tentative distance from the start.
