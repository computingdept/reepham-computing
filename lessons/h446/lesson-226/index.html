Tree traversal: post-order and breadth-first

By the end, you can…
  • Trace left-right-root post-order traversal
  • Trace level-order breadth-first traversal using a queue
  • Distinguish traversal output on the same tree

NEW LEARNING · CODE FIRST

Study the code and predict the state

// C# breadth-first traversal of an existing tree:
var queue = new Queue<Node>();
queue.Enqueue(root);
while(queue.Count > 0) {
  Node node=queue.Dequeue();
  Console.WriteLine(node.Key);
  if(node.Left != null) queue.Enqueue(node.Left);
  if(node.Right != null) queue.Enqueue(node.Right);
}
// Post-order instead visits left, right, root.
STEP 1 OF 3

Depth-first post-order

Visit an entire left subtree, then right subtree, and finally the current node.

STEP 2 OF 3

Breadth-first level order

Start with the root in a queue. Dequeue one node, visit it and enqueue each of its children.

STEP 3 OF 3

Compare output

Same structure yields different orders: 2,6,4,12,8 versus 8,4,12,2,6.

I DO · TEACHER MODEL

Find post-order traversal of root 10, children 5 and 15, with 5 having left child 2.

Order = LEFT → RIGHT → ROOT.
STEP 1 OF 2

Predict first

Visit 5's left child 2 first, then node 5.

STEP 2 OF 2

Model the reasoning

Visit right child 15, then root 10. Output 2,5,15,10.

WE DO · DIFFERENT GUIDED PROBLEM

Find breadth-first traversal of root 20, children 10 and 30, with 10 having right child 15.

Use a FIFO queue: dequeue, visit, enqueue children.
STEP 1 OF 2

Predict first

Visit root 20, enqueue 10 and 30.

STEP 2 OF 2

Model the reasoning

Visit 10 and enqueue child 15, then visit 30; final 15 gives 20,10,30,15.

Foundation · 3 marks · QUESTION 1 OF 3

Root 6 has left 3 and right 9. Give post-order.

WORKED ANSWER

[3] Visit left 3 [1], right 9 [1], root 6 [1]; 3,9,6.

Core · 5 marks · QUESTION 2 OF 3

Tree root 12, left 5 with children 2 and 8, right 17. Give breadth-first and post-order traversals.

WORKED ANSWER

[5] Breadth-first 12,5,17,2,8 [2]; post-order 2,8,5,17,12 [3].

Stretch · 6 marks · QUESTION 3 OF 3

Write pseudocode to traverse a binary tree breadth-first. Explain why a queue rather than a stack is used.

WORKED ANSWER

[6] Enqueue(root) [1]; while queue nonempty [1], dequeue and visit [1], enqueue non-null left then right children [2]; queue maintains FIFO order across levels [1].

TERM 1 OF 5

post-order

Traverse left, right, then root.

TERM 2 OF 5

breadth-first

Visit vertices level by level.

TERM 3 OF 5

FIFO queue

First-in-first-out structure used by BFS.

TERM 4 OF 5

leaf

Node without children.

TERM 5 OF 5

traversal

Systematically visit each node in a structure.
