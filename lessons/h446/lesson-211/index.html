Read the code or problem

Stack: Push(3), Push(6), Pop(), Push(9)
Queue: Enqueue(A), Enqueue(B), Dequeue()

STEP 1 OF 3

Retrieve operations

A stack pops its newest item; a queue dequeues its oldest item.

STEP 2 OF 3

Trace changing state

After Push 3, Push 6, Pop, Push 9, the stack is [3,9]. After Enqueue A,B then Dequeue, the queue is [B].

STEP 3 OF 3

Apply the BST rule

Insert 40 first, 20 left, 60 right, then 30 as the right child of 20.

I DO · MODEL

Trace stack operations Push(2), Push(7), Pop(), Push(5).

Bottom → top; every Pop removes the top item.
STEP 1 OF 3

Predict first

Start with [2,7]. The top of the stack is 7.

STEP 2 OF 3

Check the method

Pop removes 7, leaving [2].

STEP 3 OF 3

Check the method

Push 5 gives [2,5]; the top is now 5.

WE DO · DIFFERENT EXAMPLE

Trace queue operations Enqueue(X), Enqueue(Y), Dequeue(), Enqueue(Z).

Front → back; remove the oldest item.
STEP 1 OF 3

Predict first

After the two enqueues, the queue is [X,Y].

STEP 2 OF 3

Check the method

Dequeue removes X, leaving [Y].

STEP 3 OF 3

Check the method

Enqueue Z produces [Y,Z]. The next dequeue removes Y.

EXAM QUESTION 1 OF 3 · 2 MARKS · 2 MINUTES

Two operations run in order: push("t"); push("s"). State the contents of the occupied positions and the final pointer. [2]

GIVEN INFORMATION

A school's coding competition uses an array-based stack called theStack with 20 positions numbered 0 to 19. The current stack has pointer = 2 and values: index 0: "k" index 1: "n" index 2: "I" All higher positions are empty.

OCR H446/02 · June 2025 · Q3(a) · RHS exam-style scenario

MODEL ANSWER · 2 MARKS

Occupied positions after both pushes: 0 "k", 1 "n", 2 "I", 3 "t", 4 "s". Final pointer = 4.

Marking: One mark for all occupied stack values in correct index order, one mark for pointer = 4.

EXAM QUESTION 2 OF 3 · 2 MARKS · 2 MINUTES

A printer controller uses an ordinary FIFO queue for jobs, but some urgent jobs must run before jobs already waiting. Explain why the basic FIFO queue does not satisfy this requirement. [2]

OCR H446/02 · June 2024 · Q4(c) · RHS exam-style scenario

MODEL ANSWER · 2 MARKS

FIFO always removes the oldest enqueued job first. New high-priority jobs are inserted at the rear, and a normal queue does not rearrange the earlier jobs or bypass them without different queue management.

Marking: One mark for FIFO order, one for the inability to insert/reorder urgent jobs ahead of those already queued.

EXAM QUESTION 3 OF 3 · 2 MARKS · 2 MINUTES

A pupil constructs a binary search tree by inserting increasing identifiers in order, causing a nearly straight chain of nodes. Explain two performance consequences of a badly unbalanced BST compared with a reasonably balanced one. [2]

OCR H446/02 · June 2025 · Q7(b) · RHS exam-style scenario

MODEL ANSWER · 2 MARKS

An unbalanced tree can be taller, so a search may require many more successive comparisons and descend through many more levels. In the worst case its height/search cost can approach O(n), compared with O(log n) for a balanced tree.

Marking: One mark for more levels/comparisons and one for a clear efficiency consequence, such as O(n) worst case versus O(log n) for a balanced tree. Equivalent correct explanations accepted.
