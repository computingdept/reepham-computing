NEW LEARNING · EXAMPLE FIRST

Read the code or problem

Stack: Push(3), Push(6), Pop(), Push(9)
Queue: Enqueue(A), Enqueue(B), Dequeue()
BST: Insert 40, 20, 60, 30
STEP 1 OF 3

Retrieve operations

A stack pops its newest item; a queue dequeues its oldest item.

STEP 2 OF 3

Trace changing state

After Push 3, Push 6, Pop, Push 9, the stack is [3,9]. After Enqueue A,B then Dequeue, the queue is [B].

STEP 3 OF 3

Apply the BST rule

Insert 40 first, 20 left, 60 right, then 30 as the right child of 20.

I DO · MODEL

Trace stack operations Push(2), Push(7), Pop(), Push(5).

Bottom → top; every Pop removes the top item.
STEP 1 OF 3

Predict first

Start with [2,7]. The top of the stack is 7.

STEP 2 OF 3

Check the method

Pop removes 7, leaving [2].

STEP 3 OF 3

Check the method

Push 5 gives [2,5]; the top is now 5.

WE DO · DIFFERENT EXAMPLE

Trace queue operations Enqueue(X), Enqueue(Y), Dequeue(), Enqueue(Z).

Front → back; remove the oldest item.
STEP 1 OF 3

Predict first

After the two enqueues, the queue is [X,Y].

STEP 2 OF 3

Check the method

Dequeue removes X, leaving [Y].

STEP 3 OF 3

Check the method

Enqueue Z produces [Y,Z]. The next dequeue removes Y.

Foundation · 3 marks · QUESTION 1 OF 3

A stack contains [4,9] bottom-to-top. Push 2, Pop, Pop. State both removed values and remaining stack.

MODEL ANSWER

[3] First Pop removes 2 [1]; second Pop removes 9 [1]; remaining bottom-to-top [4] [1].

Core · 4 marks · QUESTION 2 OF 3

A queue receives 5,8,3. It dequeues once then enqueues 2. Give the queue and explain why its next dequeue removes 8.

MODEL ANSWER

[4] Initial front-to-back [5,8,3] [1]; dequeue 5 [1]; enqueue 2 gives [8,3,2] [1]; next removal is 8 because FIFO [1].

Challenge · 6 marks · QUESTION 3 OF 3

Insert 40,20,60,10,30,25 into a BST. Give the parent of 25, state whether it is a left/right child, and explain what happens to worst-case height when inputs arrive sorted.

MODEL ANSWER

[6] Root 40 [1]; 20 left of 40 [1]; 60 right of 40 [1]; 25 is the left child of 30 [1]. Increasingly sorted input can produce a chain [1], with search up to O(n) [1].
