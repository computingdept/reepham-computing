Original question · 1 of 6CORE · TWO INPUTS

1. Simplify a single pair

Circle the adjacent 1s. Write the simplest Boolean output Q.

Worked solution
FINAL ANSWER
Q = ¬A
Why this works

The top row has two 1s. A is 0 in both cells, so keep ¬A. B changes from 0 to 1 and is removed.

Marking: correct pair and expression.

Original question · 2 of 6CORE · TWO INPUTS

2. One square is 1

Write the AND term for the one cell containing 1.

Worked solution
FINAL ANSWER
Q = A ∧ B
Why this works

Only one cell contains 1: A = 1 and B = 1. No adjacent 1 is available, so a single-cell group is valid. Both inputs remain in the term.

Marking: correct cell and AND term.

Original question · 3 of 6DEVELOP · TWO INPUTS

3. Two overlapping pairs

Circle two pairs and combine their terms with OR (∨).

Worked solution
FINAL ANSWER
Q = ¬A ∨ B
Why this works

First pair: the whole top row has A = 0, giving ¬A. Second pair: the right column has B = 1, giving B. The two groups may overlap at A=0, B=1. Join the terms with OR.

Original question · 4 of 6DEVELOP · THREE INPUTS

4. Find the four-cell group

A is the row. Columns BC are 00, 01, 11, 10. Circle the four 1s and simplify Q.

Worked solution
FINAL ANSWER
Q = C
Why this works

All four grouped cells have C = 1, since the BC labels are 01 and 11. A changes between rows and B changes between columns. Keep C only.

Original question · 5 of 6APPLY · THREE INPUTS

5. Reduce a pair

Circle both 1s. Which two variables stay 1? Write Q.

Worked solution
FINAL ANSWER
Q = A ∧ C
Why this works

Both output 1s lie in row A=1, columns BC=01 and BC=11. C stays 1 while B changes, giving A ∧ C.

Original question · 6 of 6CHALLENGE · ADAPTED OCR SCENARIO

6. Popcorn voucher

E = voucher, F = soft drink, G = water. The offer is E AND (F OR G). Find two useful pairs and express R as the OR of two AND terms. Adapted from OCR Boolean Algebra Q2(b).

Worked solution
FINAL ANSWER
R = (E ∧ F) ∨ (E ∧ G)
Why this works

In row E=1, pair columns FG=11 and 10 to get E ∧ F. Pair columns FG=01 and 11 to get E ∧ G. These pairs overlap at FG=11. Both terms are needed to cover all three 1s.

Equivalent factored form: E ∧ (F ∨ G).
