Evaluate SSD versus HDD for a mobile video editor.

SSD: faster random access and no rotating platter; HDD: often lower cost per GB. Choose SSD for active editing, while maintaining backups.

Explain the worked method

A strong answer explains mechanical access, portability and the need for backups.

Evaluate cloud-based coursework storage against USB-only storage for a school.

Cloud: convenient multi-device access and managed permissions. USB: offline access, but loss/damage risk.

Explain the worked method

Match security, home connectivity and backup provision to the school context.

EXAM QUESTION 1 OF 2 · 9 MARKS · 9 MINUTES

A nationwide animal-welfare charity needs storage for its main file server, employee laptops and long-term nightly backups. It is choosing magnetic hard disks/tape and solid-state drives. Discuss their strengths and weaknesses for all THREE uses, including speed, durability, power, storage capacity, unit cost, endurance and practical reliability, and reach justified decisions. [9]

OCR H446/01 · November 2021 · Q1(c)* · RHS exam-style scenario

MODEL ANSWER · 9 MARKS

Solid-state devices provide low access latency, no moving parts and lower power/shock risk, useful for laptops and active server workloads, but large capacities can cost more and have finite write endurance. Magnetic disks often offer large inexpensive capacity for central storage; tape can be suitable for sequential/off-site backups but has slow random retrieval. Recommend by each use, considering resilience, cost per GB, restoration speed, and mixed/hybrid designs.

Marking: Teacher-assessed nine-mark levels: detailed and balanced context-specific recommendations 7–9; sound tradeoff discussion 4–6; basic statements 1–3; 0 no credit. Teacher marking, not keyword scoring.

EXAM QUESTION 2 OF 2 · 9 MARKS · 9 MINUTES

An arts centre currently has 150 members and expects membership to grow to 10,000. It must record members, workshop bookings and tutor appointments. Discuss the relative merits of a flat-file and a relational database. Compare their features, advantages and disadvantages, and justify which approach would be more suitable as the centre grows. [9]

OCR H446/01 · June 2024 · Q7* · RHS exam-style scenario

MODEL ANSWER · 9 MARKS

Flat files use one table and are straightforward to set up, but repeating members in each booking creates redundancy, update anomalies, slower searches and poor expansion. A relational database uses separate related tables linked through keys, reducing duplication and enabling maintenance, access controls and further tables, but requires design, normalisation and a DBMS. A relational system suits 10,000 members and many bookings better, though a flat file could work initially at 150 members.

Marking: Holistic 9-mark teacher assessment: high 7–9 thorough balanced applied comparison and justified conclusion; middle 4–6 sound but underdeveloped; low 1–3 limited; no credit 0.
