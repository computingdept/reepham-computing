EXAMPLE FIRST

Read, predict and explain

// Seven buckets indexed 0..6
int Hash(int key) => key % 7;
// Insert 9 → index 2
// Insert 16 → index 2 (collision), probe 3
// Insert 23 → index 2, then 3, then 4
STEP 1 OF 3

Calculate the index

9 MOD 7 = 2, so put 9 into bucket 2.

STEP 2 OF 3

Detect a collision

16 MOD 7 is also 2. The bucket is occupied, so move to the next bucket, 3.

STEP 3 OF 3

Probe until empty

23 also hashes to 2; buckets 2 and 3 are full, so place it in 4.

I DO · MODEL

Insert 6, 11, 16 into five buckets using h(k)=k MOD 5 and linear probing.

6 MOD 5 = 1; 11 MOD 5 = 1; 16 MOD 5 = 1
STEP 1 OF 3

Predict and explain

Insert 6 at index 1, because 6 MOD 5 = 1.

STEP 2 OF 3

Check and extend

11 collides at 1, so store it at 2.

STEP 3 OF 3

Check and extend

16 collides at 1 and 2, so store it at 3.

WE DO · DIFFERENT GUIDED PROBLEM

Insert 8, 13, 18 into five buckets using h(k)=k MOD 5.

8 MOD 5 = 3; 13 MOD 5 = 3; 18 MOD 5 = 3
STEP 1 OF 3

Predict and explain

8 goes into bucket 3.

STEP 2 OF 3

Check and extend

13 collides at 3 and probes to 4.

STEP 3 OF 3

Check and extend

18 collides at 3 and 4; linear probing wraps around to 0, which is empty, so 18 goes into bucket 0.

Foundation · QUESTION 1 OF 3

Using h(k)=k MOD 7, find the starting index for 24.

MODEL ANSWER

24 MOD 7 = 3.

Core · QUESTION 2 OF 3

Insert 4, 9, 14 into five empty buckets using h(k)=k MOD 5 and linear probing. Give final indexes.

MODEL ANSWER

All hash to 4. Insert 4 at index 4; 9 probes to 0; 14 probes past occupied 4 and 0 to index 1.

Stretch · QUESTION 3 OF 3

Explain the effect of a high load factor on linear probing.

MODEL ANSWER

More occupied buckets lead to longer probe sequences, clustering and slower expected lookup and insertion.

TERM 1 OF 5

hash function

Maps a key to an initial bucket index.

TERM 2 OF 5

collision

Two keys map to the same initial bucket.

TERM 3 OF 5

linear probing

Checks successive buckets, wrapping around when needed.

TERM 4 OF 5

load factor

The proportion of occupied table buckets.

TERM 5 OF 5

clustering

A sequence of occupied buckets increasing probe lengths.

True or false?

A hash collision means two different keys have the same starting bucket.

Answer

True. A collision requires a resolution strategy such as linear probing.
