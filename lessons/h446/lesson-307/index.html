EXAMPLE: Why is 'cache makes CPU faster' weak?
Improved: cache stores recently/frequently used
instructions/data near CPU. A cache hit avoids
some slower RAM transfers. Speedup depends on
hit rate, workload and access pattern.
STEP 1 OF 3

Diagnose rather than praise vaguely

Separate inaccurate knowledge, missing context and absent justification; use an explicit improvement target.

STEP 2 OF 3

Show full method

Write intermediary calculations, trace states and correctly named registers or SQL keys.

STEP 3 OF 3

Demonstrate transfer

Apply the improved method to a different question, not a copied mark-scheme answer.

I DO · UNMARKED MODEL

Improve 'pipelining makes instructions twice as quick'.

Overlap fetch, decode and execute stages.
Throughput can rise after filling pipeline.
Stalls/branches reduce ideal overlap.
STEP 1 OF 2

Predict first

Name the mechanism—overlapping stages of different instructions—rather than speeding an individual stage.

STEP 2 OF 2

Check reasoning

State a limitation, such as dependency stalls or branch flushes.

WE DO · DIFFERENT UNMARKED EXAMPLE

Improve 'a JOIN combines tables' for an inner join on StudentID.

Student INNER JOIN Score
ON Student.StudentID=Score.StudentID
STEP 1 OF 2

Predict first

Name the matching attribute and type of join rather than a generic table combination.

STEP 2 OF 2

Check reasoning

Explain that rows with no matching StudentID in both tables are excluded.
