STEP 1 OF 5

Find the 1s

Look at the 2×2 map on the left. Three squares contain 1. We need to cover them in groups of neighbouring 1s.

STEP 2 OF 5

A square may belong to two groups

This is overlap. It is allowed when it makes the expression simpler.

2 of 5
STEP 3 OF 5

Keep the inputs that stay the same

Bottom pair: A stays 1; B changes. Keep A.

Right pair: B stays 1; A changes. Keep B.

Q = A ∨ B
3 of 5
STEP 4 OF 5

The outside columns touch

Columns go 00, 01, 11, 10 (Gray code). B stays 0; A and C change. So Q = ¬B.

4 of 5
STEP 5 OF 5

Draw gates only after simplifying

∨ means OR. ∧ means AND. ¬ means NOT.

Q = A ∨ B → one OR gate

The map tells you which gates you need. Don't guess the circuit first.

I DO · WATCH THE FULL METHOD

How do the groups become gates?

Rows A; columns BC in Gray-code order.

Map stays on the left as we work
STEP 1 · CIRCLE

Circle the two pairs

Circle the 1s. Every group makes a term.

STEP 2 · COMPARE

Explain the top group

A stays 0 → ¬A. C stays 1 → C. B changes → remove B.

First term: ¬A ∧ C
STEP 3 · COMPARE

Explain the bottom group

A stays 1 → A. B stays 1 → B. C changes → remove C.

Second term: A ∧ B
STEP 4 · JOIN

Join the terms

Either group makes Q=1. Join the two terms with OR.

Q = (¬A ∧ C) ∨ (A ∧ B)
STEP 5 · DRAW

Turn the expression into gates

NOT A, then AND with C. AND A with B. Finally OR the outputs. Draw the proper gate symbols.
