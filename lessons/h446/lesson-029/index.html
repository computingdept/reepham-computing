QUESTION 1 OF 4

LAST LESSON · 028

In a Karnaugh-map pair, A stays 0 and B changes between 0 and 1. What is the simplified term?

ANSWER

¬A

A stays 0, so retain NOT A (¬A). B changes, so remove B.

QUESTION 2 OF 4

EARLIER · BUBBLE SORT

Show the array after one complete left-to-right pass of ascending bubble sort on [8, 3, 6, 2].

ANSWER

[3, 6, 2, 8]

Swap 8 and 3 → [3, 8, 6, 2]; swap 8 and 6 → [3, 6, 8, 2]; swap 8 and 2 → [3, 6, 2, 8]. The largest value is now last.

QUESTION 3 OF 4

EARLIER · 3D ARRAYS

C#: int[,,] scores = new int[2, 3, 4]; How many values can it store? What does scores.GetLength(2) return?

ANSWER

24 values; GetLength(2) = 4

2 layers × 3 rows × 4 columns = 24 values. Dimension 2 is the third dimension (columns).

QUESTION 4 OF 4

CHALLENGE · FLOATING POINT

Add 01000000 0011 and 01000000 0010. Format: 8-bit two’s-complement fractional mantissa + 4-bit two’s-complement exponent. Show exponent alignment and the final normalised result.

ANSWER

01100000 0011 (denary 6)

Align to exponent 3 by shifting the second mantissa right: 01000000 → 00100000. Add the mantissas: 01000000 + 00100000 = 01100000. The exponent remains 0011. This is normalised: 0.75 × 2³ = 6.

TERM 1 OF 8

efficient group

DEFINITION

A group of 1, 2, 4, 8 or 16 neighbouring 1s.

TERM 2 OF 8

overlap

DEFINITION

The same 1 can appear in two different groups.

TERM 3 OF 8

wraparound

DEFINITION

The left/right edges touch, and the top/bottom edges touch.

TERM 4 OF 8

literal

DEFINITION

An input letter, such as A, or its NOT, ¬A.

TERM 5 OF 8

product term

DEFINITION

An expression made with AND, such as A ∧ B.

TERM 6 OF 8

simplified expression

DEFINITION

The same output with fewer variables or gate inputs.

TERM 7 OF 8

equivalent

DEFINITION

Two expressions that give the same output for every input.

TERM 8 OF 8

gate plan

DEFINITION

Instructions showing which gates are needed and how to connect them.

How do two groups become a circuit?

Rows A; columns BC in Gray-code order.

The SAME two coloured groups stay visible
STEP 1 · GROUP

Find the two pairs of 1s

ORANGE · TOP: circle columns 01 and 11 where A = 0.

BLUE · BOTTOM: circle columns 11 and 10 where A = 1.

Follow the same coloured groups on the map on the left. Each pair becomes one term.

STEP 2 · ORANGE PAIR

Keep ¬A and C

A = 0 in both squares, so write ¬A. C = 1 in both, so keep C. B changes, so remove B.

First term: ¬A ∧ C
STEP 3 · BLUE PAIR

Keep A and B

A = 1 in both squares, so keep A. B = 1 in both, so keep B. C changes, so remove C.

Second term: A ∧ B
STEP 4 · COMBINE

Join the two pairs with OR

Either coloured pair makes Q = 1. AND the inputs inside each pair; OR the two results together.

Q = (¬A ∧ C) ∨ (A ∧ B)
STEP 5 · DRAW

Draw the actual logic gates

Q = (¬A ∧ C) ∨ (A ∧ B)

Trace it: A goes through NOT for the orange pair; A also goes directly to the blue pair. Each pair feeds an AND gate. OR the two AND outputs to get Q.

CHECK · GROUP

2. Compare your circles

First group: top-left 2×2. Second group: the first and last columns in the bottom two rows form one wraparound group of four.

YOUR TURN · WRITE

3. Simplify the group(s)

Write the fixed inputs for each group. Make each term, join with OR, and plan the gates.

CHECK · EXPLAIN

4. Why is this correct?

First group: A=0, C=0 → ¬A ∧ ¬C. Second: A=1, D=0 → A ∧ ¬D. P = (¬A ∧ ¬C) ∨ (A ∧ ¬D). In the first group B and D change; in the second B and C change. Remove those changing inputs. Plan: NOT A and NOT C → AND; A and NOT D → AND; then OR both terms.
