QUESTION 1 OF 4

Last lesson

Why must floating-point exponents match before the mantissas are added directly?

ANSWER

Because corresponding mantissa bit positions must represent the same scaled place values.

Aligning the exponents puts both operands on the same scale before the mantissas are added.

QUESTION 2 OF 4

2–3 lessons back

Decode 01100000 1110 in 8+4 two’s-complement floating-point format.

ANSWER

0.1875

Mantissa 01100000 = 0.75; exponent 1110 = −2; 0.75 × 2⁻² = 0.75 ÷ 4 = 0.1875.

QUESTION 4 OF 4

Challenge

Use 8-bit two’s complement to calculate 18 − 29. Show how subtraction becomes addition.

ANSWER
+18 = 00010010
+29 = 00011101
−29 = 11100011

  00010010
+ 11100011
= 11110101

11110101 represents −11. So 18 − 29 = 18 + (−29) = −11.

Floating-point subtraction

By the end, you can…
  • align exponents and subtract by adding a negated mantissa;
  • handle positive, negative and zero differences;
  • retain working precision and normalise after cancellation.
Key vocabulary for today
NegationCancellationGuard bitsArithmetic right shiftRenormalisationUnderflow

Use a SIX-bit two’s-complement mantissa and FOUR-bit exponent throughout. The mantissa’s implied point follows its sign bit.

SUBTRACTION REFERENCE

A − B = A + (−B)

1. Align exponents
2. Keep guard bits
3. Negate B's mantissa
4. Add
5. Normalise

Negate a two’s-complement mantissa by inverting the working bits and adding 1.

Do not negate the exponent. It controls scale, not the sign of the operand.
STEP 1 OF 4

Align before subtracting

Choose a common exponent and use arithmetic right shifts to align the mantissas. Keep any low-order guard bits needed for an exact working result.

1 of 4
STEP 2 OF 4

Negate the second mantissa

At the chosen working width, invert every bit of B’s aligned mantissa and add 1.

A − B
= A + (−B)
2 of 4
STEP 3 OF 4

Add the aligned values

Sign-extend both operands to the same working width, add them, and interpret the signed result using the common exponent.

Subtracting a negative value means adding its positive counterpart.
3 of 4
STEP 4 OF 4

Handle cancellation, zero and range

A small difference may need left shifts and a reduced exponent to become normalised. Equal operands produce zero.

Use 000000 0000 for zero. A required exponent below the minimum underflows; above the maximum overflows.
4 of 4
ONE COMPLETE WORKED EXAMPLE

Subtract 1.5 from 5

5 − 1.5

Rewrite subtraction as addition of the negative value.

Worked example
STEP 1 · ALIGN

Use common exponent 3

Align both mantissas using sign-preserving right shifts.

A: 0010100
B: 0000110
STEP 2 · NEGATE B

Invert and add 1

B:        0000110
invert →  1111001
add 1  →  1111010

Negate the aligned mantissa, not the exponent.

STEP 3 · ADD

Calculate A + (−B)

   0010100
 + 1111010
 = 0001110

The working difference is 14/32 × 2³.

STEP 4 · NORMALISE & CHECK

Shift left and reduce the exponent

0001110 × 2³
→ 011100 × 2²

Final: 011100 0010

Check: 5 − 1.5 = 3.5.

Final representation = 011100 0010
