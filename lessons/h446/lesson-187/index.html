QUESTION 1 OF 4

Divide and conquer

What is meant by divide and conquer?

ANSWER

Split into smaller problems, solve them, then combine the results.

QUESTION 2 OF 4

Base case

What is a base case?

ANSWER

The condition that stops further recursive calls.

QUESTION 3 OF 4

Pivot

For ascending order, should values smaller than a pivot go before or after it?

ANSWER

Before it / to the left.

QUESTION 4 OF 4

Partitions

Why can two partitions be sorted independently once a pivot is fixed?

ANSWER

The pivot separates values into groups that belong on different sides of its final position.

Quick Sort

By the end, you can…
  • identify a pivot and partition data around it;
  • trace the recursive quick-sort process until the stopping condition is reached;
  • distinguish quick sort from merge, insertion and bubble sort.

TERM 1 OF 8

quick sort

DEFINITION

A sorting algorithm that selects a pivot, partitions the data around it and recursively sorts the partitions.

TERM 2 OF 8

pivot

DEFINITION

The chosen value used to divide the current list.

TERM 3 OF 8

partition

DEFINITION

Divide items into groups relative to the pivot.

TERM 4 OF 8

left partition

DEFINITION

The sublist containing values placed before the pivot.

TERM 5 OF 8

right partition

DEFINITION

The sublist containing values placed after the pivot.

TERM 6 OF 8

old pivot

DEFINITION

A pivot whose final position has been fixed.

TERM 7 OF 8

base case

DEFINITION

A stopping condition such as a sublist of length 1.

TERM 8 OF 8

recursion

DEFINITION

Applying quick sort again to the smaller partitions.

WORKED EXAMPLE

Model the method clearly
STEP 1

Pivot 5

smaller: 2 1 4
pivot: 5
larger: 7

7 is on the right; 2, 1 and 4 are on the left.

STEP 2

Sort the left partition

2 [1] 4
→ [ ] 1 [2 4]

Choose a pivot again and partition.

STEP 3

Continue

2 4 → pivot 4
→ [2] 4

The remaining sublists reach length 1.

STEP 4

Combine the fixed positions

1 2 4 5 7

All pivots / singletons are now in sorted order.
