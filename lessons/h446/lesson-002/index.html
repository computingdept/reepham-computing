QUESTION 4 OF 4

Can it fit?

Write 200 in unsigned 8-bit binary. A pupil says 300 will also fit in eight bits. Explain why this is incorrect.

ANSWER
200 = 128 + 64 + 8 → 11001000. Eight unsigned bits represent 0–255; 300 needs at least nine bits.

Signed integers & binary arithmetic

By the end, you can…

  • add binary integers and subtract using two’s complement;
  • identify signed overflow and justify the range of a fixed-width representation.

SIGNED INTEGER REFERENCE

Two representations

Sign magnitude:
sign | magnitude
0 = positive · 1 = negative

Two's complement weights:
−128 64 32 16 8 4 2 1

Sign magnitude range: −127 to +127

Two’s complement range: −128 to +127

Always identify the representation before interpreting the same bit pattern.
STEP 1 OF 4

Sign magnitude

The leftmost bit stores the sign. The remaining bits store the positive magnitude.

10000101
sign = 1
magnitude = 0000101 = 5
value = −5
There are two zero patterns in sign magnitude.

STEP 2 OF 4

Two’s complement

The most significant bit has a negative place value.

11111011
= −128 + 64 + 32 + 16 + 8 + 2 + 1
= −5
To negate a fixed-width value: invert all bits, then add 1.
2 of 4
STEP 3 OF 4

Add using binary carries

0 + 0 = 0
0 + 1 = 1
1 + 1 = 10
1 + 1 + 1 = 11

Keep the fixed number of result bits. A carry beyond the width is discarded.

3 of 4
STEP 4 OF 4

Overflow and subtraction

Signed overflow occurs when the mathematical result is outside the representable range. Two same-sign inputs producing an opposite-sign result is a warning sign.

For subtraction, use A − B = A + (−B). Negate B using two’s complement, then add.

ONE COMPLETE WORKED EXAMPLE

Encode −13 in two representations

+13 = 00001101

Keep the original value fixed while the method is revealed on the right.

Worked example
STEP 1 · START POSITIVE

Write +13 in eight bits

13 = 8 + 4 + 1
+13 = 00001101

Both signed representations begin from the positive magnitude.

STEP 2 · SIGN MAGNITUDE

Set the sign bit

00001101
→ 10001101

Keep the seven magnitude bits and change the leftmost sign bit to 1.

STEP 3 · TWO’S COMPLEMENT

Invert, then add 1

00001101
invert → 11110010
add 1  → 11110011
STEP 4 · CHECK

Decode the result

−128 + 64 + 32 + 16 + 2 + 1 = −13
Two’s complement −13 = 11110011

Invert-and-add-one negates a value in a fixed width.

Compare your method

   01100100   (+100)
 + 00101000   (+40)
 ----------
   10001100   (−116 if decoded)

The mathematical answer is 140, above +127. Two positive inputs produced a negative stored result: signed overflow. There is no carry out, showing why carry out alone is not a signed-overflow test.

Conversely, −1 + 1 produces 1 00000000. Discard the carry: the stored result is zero and there is no signed overflow.
