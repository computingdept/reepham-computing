WORKED REPRESENTATION FIRST

Read and interpret

BEFORE   0.0110₂ × 2⁴  = 6₁₀
SHIFT    0.1100₂ × 2³  = 6₁₀
          ↑ left       ↓ 1 exponent
STEP 1 OF 3

Why is 0.0110 not normalised?

For a positive two's-complement fractional mantissa, a normalised value begins with 01, not 00.

STEP 2 OF 3

Shift and compensate

Shift 0.0110 left one place to 0.1100. Decrease the exponent from 4 to 3 to compensate.

STEP 3 OF 3

Verify the value

0.0110₂ is 0.375; 0.375 × 16 = 6. 0.1100₂ is 0.75; 0.75 × 8 = 6.

I DO · TEACHER MODEL

Normalise positive 0.0011₂ × 2⁵.

0.0011 × 2⁵
→ 0.0110 × 2⁴
→ 0.1100 × 2³
STEP 1 OF 3

First shift

0.0011 becomes 0.0110; reduce exponent 5 → 4. It still begins 00.

STEP 2 OF 3

Second shift

0.0110 becomes 0.1100; reduce exponent 4 → 3. The first two bits are now 01.

STEP 3 OF 3

Check

Both represent 6 in decimal, so normalisation preserved the value.

WE DO · DIFFERENT GUIDED PROBLEM

Normalise positive 0.0101₂ × 2⁴.

0.0101 × 2⁴
→ ? × 2?
STEP 1 OF 2

Predict the shift

Shift the mantissa left one position: 0.0101 → 0.1010.

STEP 2 OF 2

Adjust and check

Exponent decreases from 4 to 3. Both representations equal decimal 5.

Foundation · 1 OF 3

Identify mantissa and exponent in 0.1100₂ × 2³.

MODEL ANSWER

Mantissa 0.1100₂; exponent 3.

Core · 2 OF 3

Normalise positive 0.0010₂ × 2⁵.

MODEL ANSWER

0.0010 × 2⁵ → 0.0100 × 2⁴ → 0.1000 × 2³.

Stretch · 3 OF 3

Why can normalisation improve precision without increasing the number of mantissa bits?

MODEL ANSWER

Redundant leading sign bits are removed so available mantissa positions hold more significant information. Exponent adjustment preserves the represented value.

A two's-complement fractional mantissa with different first two bits.

True or false?

Shifting 0.0110 × 2⁴ left to 0.1100 × 2⁴ preserves its value.

Model answer

False. To preserve the value, the exponent must decrease to 3.
