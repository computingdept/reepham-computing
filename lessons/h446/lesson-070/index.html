Lesson 070 · Heuristics and Performance Modelling

By the end you can…
  • A heuristic is a useful estimate or rule of thumb that guides decisions when complete exploration is expensive.
  • A locally cheapest edge does not necessarily lead to a globally cheapest route; evaluate candidate routes as complete paths.
  • Performance modelling estimates time, memory or cost under assumptions. A predicted result requires checking against real hardware and workloads.

STEP 1 OF 3

Define and predict

A heuristic is a useful estimate or rule of thumb that guides decisions when complete exploration is expensive.

STEP 2 OF 3

Explain

A locally cheapest edge does not necessarily lead to a globally cheapest route; evaluate candidate routes as complete paths.

STEP 3 OF 3

Evaluate

Performance modelling estimates time, memory or cost under assumptions. A predicted result requires checking against real hardware and workloads.

I DO · WORKED

Choose cheaper complete path: X→Y→G is 3+9; X→Z→G is 6+2.

Via Y costs12; via Z costs8, so Z wins despite first edge 6>3.
WORKED EXPLANATION

Follow the reasoning

Calculate the entire feasible route rather than the next immediate edge.

WE DO · DIFFERENT CASE

Estimate processing 600 records at 50 records/s, then discuss what model ignores.

600/50=12s under constant-rate assumptions; ignores setup/IO/congestion.
WORKED EXPLANATION

Follow the reasoning

A performance model is a simplified prediction, not a guaranteed elapsed time.

True or false?

A local greedy choice always produces an optimal complete path.

Answer

False. Future edge costs can reverse the choice.
