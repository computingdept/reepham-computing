Lesson 095 · CPU Pipelining: Overlap and Hazards

By the end you can…
  • Pipelining splits instruction processing into stages; several instructions can occupy different stages during the same clock cycle.
  • With four independent instructions and three one-cycle stages, ideal completion is 3+4−1=6 cycles; serial non-overlap takes 4×3=12.
  • Data dependencies, branching and resource conflicts can stall or flush a pipeline. Throughput benefits need ideal assumptions, not automatic latency reductions.

I DO · WORKED MODEL

Three instructions pass through a two-stage pipeline. Compute ideal completion cycles.

MODEL EXPLANATION

Explain the mechanism

2+3−1=4 cycles; serial baseline 2×3=6 cycles.

The second task starts stage one while first advances to stage two.

WE DO · DIFFERENT QUESTION

Five independent instructions use a four-stage ideal pipeline. Calculate completion.

MODEL EXPLANATION

Explain the mechanism

4+5−1=8 cycles, compared with 4×5=20 serial stage cycles.

Assumes equal one-cycle stages, no resource conflicts or stalls.
