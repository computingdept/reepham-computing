Signed binary and shifts

By the end, you can…
  • Encode signed integers using 8-bit two's complement
  • Trace logical and arithmetic shifts
  • Recognise loss of information and overflow

Key vocabulary for today

two's complementsign bitlogical shiftarithmetic right shiftoverflow
CODE / EXAMPLE FIRST

Predict what happens

+13 = 00001101
invert → 11110010
add 1 → 11110011 (−13)
STEP 1 OF 3

POSITIVE 13

+13 written with 8 bits is 00001101.

STEP 2 OF 3

INVERT BITS

Invert each bit to make 11110010.

STEP 3 OF 3

ADD ONE

Add one to get 11110011, the 8-bit two's-complement representation of −13.

I DO · TEACHER MODEL

Find −9 in 8-bit two's complement.

+9  = 00001001
NOT = 11110110
+1  = 11110111
STEP 1 OF 2

Predict first

Write +9 in 8 bits and invert all the bits.

STEP 2 OF 2

Model the result

Add 1 to the inverted bits: 11110111 represents −9.

WE DO · DIFFERENT GUIDED EXAMPLE

Convert −6 into 8-bit two's complement.

+6  = 00000110
NOT = ?
+1  = ?
STEP 1 OF 2

Predict first

+6 = 00000110. Invert to get 11111001.

STEP 2 OF 2

Model the result

Add 1: 11111010 is the representation of −6.

Foundation · 1 OF 3

Write −5 as 8-bit two's complement.

MODEL ANSWER

+5=00000101; inverted=11111010; add 1=11111011.

Core · 2 OF 3

Unsigned 8-bit 00110100 is shifted logically right one place. Give the result and decimal value.

MODEL ANSWER

00011010, which is 26 (52 / 2).

Stretch · 3 OF 3

11110100 represents −12 in two's complement. Arithmetically shift right one place and explain the new left bit.

MODEL ANSWER

11111010, representing −6. The sign bit 1 is copied into the new leftmost position.

TERM 1 OF 5

two's complement

Fixed-width signed binary representation.

TERM 2 OF 5

sign bit

Leftmost bit indicating sign in two's complement.

TERM 3 OF 5

logical shift

Shift with zero bits entering vacated positions.

TERM 4 OF 5

arithmetic right shift

Shift right while extending the sign bit.

TERM 5 OF 5

overflow

Result cannot be represented in the available bit width.

True or false?

Arithmetic right shift of a negative two's-complement value inserts zero at the left.

Model answer

False. An arithmetic right shift replicates the sign bit (1 for negative values).
