Computing legislation

By the end you can…
  • Study the four Acts named in the H446 specification for exam terminology, but do not confuse the historical Data Protection Act 1998 with current law.
  • Current UK personal-data processing is principally governed by UK GDPR and the Data Protection Act 2018, with later amendments. Lawful processing, fairness, transparency and data minimisation are key principles.
  • The Computer Misuse Act concerns unauthorised computer access and attacks; copyright law protects qualifying creative works; RIPA addresses particular investigatory powers and safeguards, not a general licence for anyone to monitor communications.

I DO · MODEL

A school publishes identifiable pupil progress data online without appropriate lawful basis. Which legal area is central?

Personal data + publication → UK GDPR/DPA 2018.
OCR H446 also names historic DPA 1998.
STEP 1 OF 2

Predict first

Identifiable progress records are personal data; assess a lawful basis, purpose, fairness, security and privacy duties.

STEP 2 OF 2

Explain

For current UK law use UK GDPR and DPA 2018, while recognising the older Act named in OCR wording.

WE DO · DIFFERENT PROBLEM

Someone deliberately signs into a colleague's account without permission and copies files. Which law is directly relevant?

Unauthorised account access → Computer Misuse Act 1990.
Copying protected content may create separate issues.
STEP 1 OF 2

Predict first

The key fact is intentional access without authorisation, engaging computer misuse offences.

STEP 2 OF 2

Explain

Additional misuse of copied data may raise data-protection or copyright questions depending on the facts.

EXAM QUESTION 1 OF 4 · 3 MARKS · 3 MINUTES

At a computing-law workshop, learners study the Regulation of Investigatory Powers Act and related lawful surveillance authorisations. State THREE different kinds of investigatory access or surveillance powers that qualifying public bodies may be permitted to exercise, subject to legal safeguards. [3]

OCR H446/01 · June 2024 · Q10(a) · RHS exam-style scenario

MODEL ANSWER · 3 MARKS

Indicative categories include interception/monitoring of communications; targeted covert surveillance; lawful demands for communications traffic data; obtaining access to protected data; requiring cooperation with authorised decryption requests. Different statutory authorisations and safeguards apply.

Marking: One mark per different power recognised by the historical OCR scheme, maximum three. Do not describe unrestricted permissions.

EXAM QUESTION 2 OF 4 · 3 MARKS · 3 MINUTES

A charity registers volunteers for events and stores names, contact details and availability. State THREE distinct principles under UK data-protection rules that should guide its handling of personal information. [3]

OCR H446/01 · June 2024 · Q10(b) · RHS exam-style scenario

MODEL ANSWER · 3 MARKS

Lawfulness, fairness and transparency; purpose limitation; data minimisation; accuracy; storage limitation; integrity/security/confidentiality; accountability.

Marking: One mark per distinct appropriate principle, maximum three. The RHS answer uses modern UK GDPR terms; reconcile with OCR's dated 2024 wording at final source review.

EXAM QUESTION 3 OF 4 · 2 MARKS · 2 MINUTES

A museum streams independent filmmakers' content to paid members. Explain TWO ways copyright protection affects the museum. [2]

OCR H446/01 · June 2022 · Q2(e) · RHS exam-style scenario

MODEL ANSWER · 2 MARKS

Films are generally copyright-protected when created, and the service needs an appropriate licence/authorisation for hosting or streaming; contractual restrictions may apply.

Marking: One for owner/protection and one for permission/licensing or appropriate restriction.

EXAM QUESTION 4 OF 4 · 9 MARKS · 9 MINUTES

A student interested in cybersecurity discovers weaknesses while experimenting with computer systems they do not own. Some argue that exploring vulnerabilities for learning should not be treated as a crime when there is no intent to cause harm. Discuss this argument with reference to the UK Computer Misuse Act, unauthorised access, consent, responsible disclosure, possible harms and the distinction between lawful research and unlawful activity. Reach a justified judgement. [9]

OCR H446/01 · November 2021 · Q4* · RHS exam-style scenario

MODEL ANSWER · 9 MARKS

The Computer Misuse Act addresses unauthorised access and related offences; lack of intent to cause damage does not necessarily provide permission. Researchers can use systems they own, explicitly approved testing environments or authorised bug-bounty scopes; testing without permission can invade privacy or modify logs/data. Balance the security value of discovering weaknesses with the need to protect owners and users and the possibility of responsible disclosure. Verify current statutory details before publication.

Marking: Teacher-marked nine-mark levels: 7–9 detailed and balanced legal/ethical application; 4–6 sound but incomplete, 1–3 basic, 0 none. No automatic keyword scoring.
