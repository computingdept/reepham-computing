Computing legislation

By the end you can…
  • Study the four Acts named in the H446 specification for exam terminology, but do not confuse the historical Data Protection Act 1998 with current law.
  • Current UK personal-data processing is principally governed by UK GDPR and the Data Protection Act 2018, with later amendments. Lawful processing, fairness, transparency and data minimisation are key principles.
  • The Computer Misuse Act concerns unauthorised computer access and attacks; copyright law protects qualifying creative works; RIPA addresses particular investigatory powers and safeguards, not a general licence for anyone to monitor communications.

I DO · MODEL

A school publishes identifiable pupil progress data online without appropriate lawful basis. Which legal area is central?

Personal data + publication → UK GDPR/DPA 2018.
OCR H446 also names historic DPA 1998.
STEP 1 OF 2

Predict first

Identifiable progress records are personal data; assess a lawful basis, purpose, fairness, security and privacy duties.

STEP 2 OF 2

Explain

For current UK law use UK GDPR and DPA 2018, while recognising the older Act named in OCR wording.

WE DO · DIFFERENT PROBLEM

Someone deliberately signs into a colleague's account without permission and copies files. Which law is directly relevant?

Unauthorised account access → Computer Misuse Act 1990.
Copying protected content may create separate issues.
STEP 1 OF 2

Predict first

The key fact is intentional access without authorisation, engaging computer misuse offences.

STEP 2 OF 2

Explain

Additional misuse of copied data may raise data-protection or copyright questions depending on the facts.
