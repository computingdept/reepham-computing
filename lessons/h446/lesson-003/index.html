ANSWER

QUESTION 4 OF 4

Add and check the range

Add 01110000 and 00100000 as eight-bit two’s-complement values. State the stored result and explain whether signed overflow occurs.

ANSWER
10010000. 112 + 32 = 144, outside −128…127. Signed overflow occurs; the stored pattern would decode to −112.

Hexadecimal, shifts & masks

By the end, you can…
  • convert positive integers between hexadecimal, binary and denary;
  • apply fixed-width logical shifts and explain their limits;
  • use AND, OR and XOR masks to inspect, clear, set and toggle bits.

AND preserve/clear · OR set · XOR toggle

Hex is a shorter human-readable way to write the same bit pattern. It does not reduce the number of stored bits.
STEP 1 OF 4

Hexadecimal links directly to binary

Each hexadecimal digit maps to exactly four bits because four bits have 16 possible patterns.

6D₁₆
6 → 0110
D → 1101
= 01101101₂
1 of 4
STEP 2 OF 4

Logical shifts move bits

A logical shift inserts zeros into the empty positions. Bits leaving the fixed width are lost.

Left by k can multiply an unsigned value by 2ᵏ only if significant bits are not lost. Right by k divides and discards any remainder.
2 of 4
STEP 3 OF 4

AND and OR masks

AND: mask 1 preserves a bit; mask 0 clears it.

OR: mask 1 sets a bit; mask 0 preserves it.

10100101
AND 00001111
=   00000101
3 of 4
STEP 4 OF 4

XOR toggles selected bits

XOR produces 1 when the two input bits differ. A mask bit of 1 flips the corresponding data bit; 0 leaves it unchanged.

Bit 0 is the rightmost bit. Bit 7 is the leftmost bit in an 8-bit value.

ONE COMPLETE WORKED EXAMPLE

Link denary, hexadecimal and binary

173₁₀ → ?₁₆ → ?₂

Convert the same value through all three number bases.

Worked example
STEP 1 · DIVIDE BY 16

Find the hex digits

173 ÷ 16 = 10 remainder 13

The quotient gives the first hex digit; the remainder gives the second.

STEP 2 · MAP THE DIGITS

Convert 10 and 13 to hex

10 = A
13 = D

173₁₀ = AD₁₆
STEP 3 · HEX → BINARY

Use four bits per hex digit

A → 1010
D → 1101

AD₁₆ = 10101101₂
STEP 4 · CHECK

Return to denary

(10 × 16) + 13 = 173

128 + 32 + 8 + 4 + 1 = 173
173₁₀ = AD₁₆ = 10101101₂

For binary → hex, group bits from the right in fours and pad the left group with zeros if needed.
