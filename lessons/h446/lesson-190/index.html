QUESTION 1 OF 4

Tentative distance

What is a tentative distance?

ANSWER

The best currently known cost from the start to a node.

QUESTION 2 OF 4

Update

When should a stored distance be replaced?

ANSWER

When a newly found route has a smaller total cost.

QUESTION 3 OF 4

Edge weight

What does an edge weight represent?

ANSWER

A cost/distance associated with traversing the edge.

QUESTION 4 OF 4

Estimate

What extra information could help a search head toward a known goal?

ANSWER

An estimate of the remaining cost/distance to the goal — a heuristic.

A* Path Finding

By the end, you can…
  • explain how A* extends the path-finding idea used in Dijkstra’s algorithm;
  • combine actual cost with heuristic cost to prioritise nodes;
  • apply the source A* method and explain the role of heuristic accuracy.

TERM 1 OF 8

A* algorithm

DEFINITION

A path-finding algorithm that combines actual path cost with a heuristic estimate toward the goal.

TERM 2 OF 8

actual cost

DEFINITION

The known cost accumulated along the route from the start node.

TERM 3 OF 8

heuristic

DEFINITION

An approximate cost from a node to the goal.

TERM 4 OF 8

total cost

DEFINITION

The value used to choose the next node after combining actual and heuristic costs.

TERM 5 OF 8

goal node

DEFINITION

The destination the path-finding algorithm is trying to reach.

TERM 6 OF 8

estimate

DEFINITION

An approximate value rather than a known path cost.

TERM 7 OF 8

priority

DEFINITION

The ordering used to decide which candidate node is explored next.

TERM 8 OF 8

shortest route

DEFINITION

The route with the lowest total path cost found to the goal.

STANDARD ALGORITHM · PATH FINDING

Actual cost + heuristic → choose lowest total

Source values from A:
B: actual 7, heuristic 6 → total 13
C: actual 3, heuristic 7 → total 10
D: actual 6, heuristic 1 → total 7

Choose D.
Key idea: Unlike Dijkstra’s source method, A* also uses an estimate from each candidate node to the goal.
STEP 1 OF 4

Keep the actual path cost

A→B = 7
A→C = 3
A→D = 6

These are known costs from the start.

STEP 2 OF 4

Add the heuristic

B: 7 + 6 = 13
C: 3 + 7 = 10
D: 6 + 1 = 7

The heuristic estimates remaining cost to the goal.

STEP 3 OF 4

Choose lowest combined total

D = 7

A* therefore explores D next in the source example.

STEP 4 OF 4

Reach the goal

A→D→E = 6 + 5 = 11

The source notes give A–D–E as the resulting shortest route.

Model the method clearly
STEP 1

Select D

lowest total = 7

The heuristic makes D the preferred next node.

STEP 2

Move from D to E

actual route cost = 6 + 5 = 11

Do not add the previous node’s heuristic into the actual path cost.

STEP 3

Goal reached

E total = 11

The source example terminates here.

STEP 4

Interpret the heuristic

better estimate → potentially less unnecessary exploration

The source notes explicitly state that effectiveness depends on heuristic accuracy.
