EXAM QUESTION 1 OF 4 · 9 MARKS · 9 MINUTES

A game developer must move computer-controlled characters around large indoor levels. It could examine every possible route, or use heuristic estimates of distance to guide choices. Discuss the value and dangers of heuristics in this situation, with examples of when a fast plausible route is preferable to a fully optimal one. [9]

OCR H446/02 · June 2024 · Q3(c)* · RHS exam-style scenario

MODEL ANSWER · 9 MARKS

A heuristic is a rule-of-thumb estimate used to rank decisions or search paths. In A*, estimated remaining cost can focus exploration, improving responsiveness in large maps; poorly chosen or overestimating heuristics may lead to wasted work or suboptimal paths depending on the algorithm's conditions. An admissible/consistent heuristic supports shortest-path guarantees with standard A* assumptions. Designers balance development effort, accuracy, compute budget, game scale and player expectations; a small fixed map may not need complex heuristics.

Marking: Teacher nine-mark holistic assessment: 7–9 detailed, correctly applied, balanced evaluation and justified choice; 4–6 sound but incomplete; 1–3 limited; 0 no credit. Avoid claiming all heuristic approaches always find the optimal route.

RHS ADDITIONAL PRACTICE · QUESTION 4 OF 4 · 3 MARKS · 3 MINUTES

A processor uses a three-stage instruction pipeline. Explain how pipelining can improve instruction throughput. [3]

RHS original follow-on question · linked to this lesson's exam practice

MODEL ANSWER · 3 MARKS

The stages of different instructions overlap: while one instruction executes, another can be decoded and another fetched. More instructions can complete per unit of time once the pipeline is filled.

Marking: One mark for splitting instructions into stages; one for overlapping different instructions; one for increased throughput. Do not require individual instruction latency to decrease.
