QUESTION 4 OF 4

Challenge

Convert AD₁₆ to binary, then logically shift the 8-bit value right once. Give the final denary value.

ANSWER

AD₁₆ = 10101101₂ → 01010110₂ = 86

A logical right shift inserts 0 on the left and discards the rightmost bit.

ADDITION REFERENCE

Align → add → normalise

−1 | ½ ¼ ⅛ ¹⁄₁₆ ¹⁄₃₂

4-bit exponent:
−8 4 2 1

Use a temporary extra sign bit for the working sum.

When aligning a negative mantissa, use an arithmetic right shift: repeat the sign bit on the left.
STEP 1 OF 4

Align the exponents

Choose the larger exponent. Increase the smaller exponent one step at a time and right-shift that mantissa once for each step.

The mantissas can only be added directly when their bit positions represent the same scaled place values.
1 of 4
STEP 2 OF 4

Preserve sign and working precision

An arithmetic right shift repeats 0 for a positive mantissa and 1 for a negative mantissa.

Keep low-order guard bits when a shift would otherwise discard a non-zero bit.

2 of 4
STEP 3 OF 4

Add using a wider working sum

Sign-extend both aligned mantissas before adding so the calculation cannot wrap into the wrong sign.

requested mantissa: 6 bits
working sum:        7 bits
3 of 4
STEP 4 OF 4

Normalise and check

If the sum is too large for the mantissa, shift right and increase the exponent. If redundant sign bits remain, shift left and decrease the exponent.

Check the exponent still fits its range. Lost low-order bits reduce precision; normalisation cannot restore them.

In 6+4 format, 32 is 010000 0110 and 0.5 is 010000 0000. Aligning the latter to exponent 6 needs six right shifts. Without guard bits, its 1 is discarded.

The exact answer is 32.5. At exponent 6, adjacent representable values differ by 2, so 32.5 cannot be stored exactly with this mantissa. Keep working precision, then apply the rounding or truncation rule specified by the question. Normalisation cannot restore lost bits.
