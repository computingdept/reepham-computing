ASSESSMENT Q1 · 15 MARKS · QUESTION 1 OF 3

(a) A stack receives 4,7,2, pops once, then pushes 5. Show its contents after the three pushes, identify the popped item, show the subsequent push, and give the final bottom-to-top stack. [4] (b) A queue receives A,B,C, dequeues twice, then enqueues D. Show the initial queue, identify each item removed, and give its final front-to-rear sequence. [4] (c) Insert 40,20,60,30,10 into an empty BST. State the root, the root's left and right children, and the parent of 30. [4] (d) Explain one limitation of an unbalanced BST. [3]

MODEL ANSWER

MARK SCHEME Q1 /15: (a) Show [4,7,2] [1], remove 2 [1], push 5 [1], final [4,7,5] [1]. (b) [A,B,C] [1], remove A [1], remove B [1], final [C,D] [1]. (c) root 40 [1], 20 left child [1], 60 right child [1], parent of 30 is 20 [1]. (d) unbalanced trees may become chains [1], causing long traversal [1] and O(n) worst-case search [1].

ASSESSMENT Q2 · 15 MARKS · QUESTION 2 OF 3

(a) Trace the first two complete ascending bubble-sort passes on [6,2,5,1]. Show each array state after a swap, identify any comparison that causes no swap, and give the final array after Pass 2. [6] (b) Explain why binary search requires sorted input. [3] (c) Compare worst-case time complexity of linear and binary search and explain the difference. [6]

MODEL ANSWER

MARK SCHEME Q2 /15: (a) Pass 1: swap 6 and 2 → [2,6,5,1] [1]; swap 6 and 5 → [2,5,6,1] [1]; swap 6 and 1 → [2,5,1,6] [1]. Pass 2: comparison 2 and 5 gives no swap [1]; swap 5 and 1 → [2,1,5,6] [1]; state the Pass 2 final array as [2,1,5,6] and note that it still requires sorting [1]. (b) midpoint ordering permits discarding half [1], assumes all smaller values are on one side [1], and fails without this ordering [1]. (c) linear O(n) [2] may visit every value [1]; binary O(log n) [2] halves the candidate interval each step [1].

ASSESSMENT Q3 · 15 MARKS · QUESTION 3 OF 3

(a) Convert −13 to 8-bit two's complement showing steps. [5] (b) Normalise positive 0.0011₂ × 2⁵, showing every step. [5] (c) Simplify ¬(A ∧ ¬B), identifying both relevant rules. [5]

MODEL ANSWER

MARK SCHEME Q3 /15: (a) +13=00001101 [1], invert=11110010 [2], add1=11110011 [2]. (b) 0.0011×2⁵→0.0110×2⁴ [2], →0.1100×2³ [2], correct 01 normalisation condition [1]. (c) De Morgan changes AND to OR and negates both inputs [2]; ¬A ∨ ¬¬B [1]; double negation [1]; final ¬A ∨ B [1].
