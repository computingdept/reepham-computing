Environment, censorship and digital accessibility

By the end you can…
  • Digital infrastructure consumes electricity and hardware resources; energy demand, device lifetimes, repairability and electronic waste are part of environmental evaluation.
  • Censorship and moderation can restrict harmful content, but also threaten freedom of expression and access to information. Evaluate proportionate rules, appeals and transparency.
  • Accessible design goes beyond attractive colours: sufficient contrast, structural headings, meaningful labels, keyboard access, language/character support and alternatives to colour-only cues.

I DO · MODEL

A chart shows success only with green and failure only with red. How can you improve accessibility?

Keep colour, add SUCCESS/FAIL labels
+ distinct symbols/shapes
+ sufficient contrast.
STEP 1 OF 2

Predict first

Colour-only status is inaccessible to users with some colour-vision differences.

STEP 2 OF 2

Explain

Add words and/or shapes alongside adequate contrast; do not rely on colour as the sole signal.

WE DO · DIFFERENT PROBLEM

A school removes all comments from an online student discussion after one offensive message. Evaluate.

Alternative: targeted moderation,
clear rules, evidence and appeal.
STEP 1 OF 2

Predict first

Blanket removal prevents harmful posts but can also silence legitimate discussion and learning.

STEP 2 OF 2

Explain

Transparent, proportionate moderation with appeal and safeguarding escalation better balances harms and expression.
