I DO · TEACHER MODEL

A→B,C; B→C; C→A. Initially PR(A)=PR(B)=PR(C)=1/3; d=0.85. Calculate one iteration.

STEP 1 OF 3

A→B,C | B→C | C→A

Identify the input or diagram and predict the first result.

STEP 2 OF 3

Incoming shares 1/3,1/6,1/2

Follow the second stage and explain its importance.

STEP 3 OF 3

Result .333,.192,.475

Incoming shares A:1/3; B:1/6; C:1/6+1/3=1/2. Base (1-.85)/3=.05. New A=.3333, B=.1917, C=.4750, sum=1.0000.

WE DO · DIFFERENT CASE

A→B; B→A,C; C→A; equal initial rank and d=0.85. Which page ranks highest after one update?

STEP 1 OF 3

A→B | B→A,C | C→A

Identify the input or diagram and predict the first result.

STEP 2 OF 3

Shares 1/2,1/3,1/6

Follow the second stage and explain its importance.

STEP 3 OF 3

A=.475 highest

Incoming contributions A=1/6+1/3=1/2, B=1/3, C=1/6. Add base 0.05 and scale by 0.85: A=.4750, B=.3333, C=.1917. A ranks highest.

FOUNDATION · QUESTION 1 OF 3

Explain how PageRank uses inbound links and outbound link counts. [6]

MARK SCHEME / WORKED ANSWER

Incoming links carry rank [2], each source shares across outgoing links [2], iterative importance measures [2].

APPLICATION · QUESTION 2 OF 3

A page of rank 0.40 has two outgoing links. What undamped contribution goes down each? [4]

MARK SCHEME / WORKED ANSWER

0.40/2=0.20 to each [4].

CHALLENGE · QUESTION 3 OF 3

Compute one PageRank update for A→B,C B→C C→A with equal initial ranks and d=.85, then explain an important limitation. [8]

MARK SCHEME / WORKED ANSWER

Incoming shares [3], updated scores A=.3333, B=.1917, C=.475 [3], ignores query-text relevance/content [2].
