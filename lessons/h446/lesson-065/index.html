Lesson 065 · Computational Methods: Recognising Solvable Problems

By the end you can…
  • A solvable computational problem needs an adequately precise input representation, a finite method and a verifiable output or objective.
  • Problem recognition identifies the class of task—classification, searching, route finding, scheduling or optimisation—before selecting a method.
  • Define measurable success criteria and constraints; vague goals such as 'best route' require clarification of 'best' (distance, cost or time).

I DO · MODEL

Turn 'choose the best bus route' into a precise computational problem.

Inputs departure stop, destination, timetable, fares; criterion cheapest valid route.
WORKED EXPLANATION

Explain the decision

Cost is defined and connections must be valid; output stops and total fare.

WE DO · NEW SCENARIO

Turn 'pick the fairest lunch queue order' into a computable FIFO rule.

Input ordered arrival timestamps and ticket IDs; output same order, oldest first; handle ties by sequence number.
WORKED EXPLANATION

Explain the decision

An ordering rule makes fairness measurable and repeatable within the chosen model.
