QUESTION 1 OF 4 · LAST LESSON · L031 · FLIP-FLOPS

Retrieval

A rising-edge-triggered D-type flip-flop has Q = 0 and D = 1. What happens to Q when the next rising edge occurs?

MODEL ANSWER

Q becomes 1 at the rising edge because the flip-flop samples D. It then holds that value until the next triggering edge.

QUESTION 2 OF 4 · EARLIER · L029 · K-MAPS

Retrieval

Why do we group neighbouring 1s together on a Karnaugh map?

MODEL ANSWER

We group neighbouring 1s to simplify the Boolean expression. Variables that change within the group can be removed.

QUESTION 3 OF 4 · OLDER · L020 · STACKS

Retrieval

Push A, then B, then C onto an empty stack. Pop once and then push D. Which item is at the top, and what is the remaining stack from top to bottom?

MODEL ANSWER

D is at the top. From top to bottom the stack is D, B, A. C was removed by the pop.

QUESTION 4 OF 4 · CHALLENGE · EARLIER BINARY REPRESENTATION

Challenge

Using 8-bit two’s complement, write −13 and explain why simply putting 1 before the 7-bit magnitude is not correct.

MODEL ANSWER

13 = 00001101₂. Invert to 11110010₂, then add 1: 11110011₂. Prefixing the magnitude gives sign-and-magnitude (10001101₂), not two’s complement.
