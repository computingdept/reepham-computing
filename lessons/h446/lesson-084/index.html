Lesson 084 · A* Search: g, h and f Scores

By the end you can…
  • A* orders candidate states using f(n)=g(n)+h(n), where g is known cost from the start and h estimates remaining cost to the goal.
  • A heuristic that never overestimates remaining true cost is admissible; with suitable search implementation/assumptions this supports optimality.
  • Track open candidates, their g/h/f and predecessors. An overestimating heuristic or incorrect update can make search miss the shortest path.

Key vocabulary

A*g(n)h(n)f(n)

I DO · WORKED

Given P→Q3, Q→R2, P→R8; h(Q)=2,h(R)=0. What is f(Q) after P?

g(Q)=3, h(Q)=2, f(Q)=5; direct R has g8 f8.

Method and result

g(Q)=3, h(Q)=2, f(Q)=5; direct R has g8 f8.

WE DO · DIFFERENT VALUES

Given X→Y4, X→Z10, Y→Z3, h(Y)=3,h(Z)=0; compare f after X.

Y f4+3=7; Z f10+0=10. Expand Y; improves Z to cost7.

Method and result

Y f4+3=7; Z f10+0=10. Expand Y; improves Z to cost7.

CHALLENGE · Q3/3

Compare A* with Dijkstra when h=0 everywhere, and explain risk of overestimation. [6]

MARK SCHEME / WORKING

With h0, f=g [2], same priority basis as Dijkstra [2], overestimating h can prioritise unsuitable paths and undermine optimality [2].
