NEW LEARNING · EXAMPLE FIRST

Read the code or problem

FEEDBACK WORKFLOW
1. Mark Q1–Q3 out of 15 each.
2. Identify the weakest method.
3. Rewrite the solution, not just the result.
4. Complete a different check question.
STEP 1 OF 3

Diagnose by question

A low Q1 mark suggests data structure operations; Q2 suggests algorithm traces and efficiency; Q3 suggests representation or Boolean rules.

STEP 2 OF 3

Model a precise correction

Correct each lost method step, including the relevant input and why the operation occurs.

STEP 3 OF 3

Retest with a different input

Do not just memorise the assessment numbers. Transfer the method to a new problem.

I DO · MODEL

A pupil claims bubble-sort pass 1 on [6,2,5,1] gives [2,5,6,1]. Diagnose the missed step.

Compare 6 with 2, then 5, then 1.
STEP 1 OF 2

Predict first

[2,5,6,1] is an intermediate state; the last comparison 6 versus 1 is still outstanding.

STEP 2 OF 2

Check the method

Swap 6 and 1. Correct pass-1 end is [2,5,1,6].

WE DO · DIFFERENT EXAMPLE

A pupil normalises 0.0010₂ × 2⁵ to 0.1000₂ × 2⁵. Identify and correct the error.

Two mantissa left shifts require two exponent decreases.
STEP 1 OF 2

Predict first

Shifting the mantissa left increases its value. The exponent must decrease by the same number of positions.

STEP 2 OF 2

Check the method

Two shifts give 0.1000₂ × 2³, not ×2⁵, and preserve the decimal value.

RETEACH Q1 · 6 MARKS · QUESTION 1 OF 3

Insert 30,15,45,22 into an empty BST. Identify the root, parent of 22 and whether 22 is a left/right child. Explain your comparison path.

MODEL ANSWER

[6] Root 30 [1]; 15 is left of 30 [1]; 45 is right of 30 [1]; 22 is greater than 15 [1] but less than 30 [1], so right child of 15 [1].

RETEACH Q2 · 6 MARKS · QUESTION 2 OF 3

Give two complete ascending bubble-sort passes on [7,4,6,2] and explain why the largest element is fixed after pass one.

MODEL ANSWER

[6] Pass1 [4,6,2,7] [2]; pass2 [4,2,6,7] [2]; adjacent swaps push larger values right [1], so the maximum 7 reaches the final position after pass one [1].

RETEACH Q3 · 6 MARKS · QUESTION 3 OF 3

Normalise positive 0.0101₂ × 2⁴ and simplify ¬(A ∨ ¬B), showing the rule at each stage.

MODEL ANSWER

[6] 0.0101→0.1010 [1]; exponent 4→3 [1]; 0.1010₂ × 2³ = decimal 5 [1]. De Morgan: ¬A ∧ ¬¬B [2], then ¬A ∧ B [1].
