LESSON OBJECTIVES

Welcome to Computing

7.1 · Introducing Computing · Digital literacy and safe working

By the end of this lesson, I can…

  1. Explain how we show respect for others, our classroom and our learning.
  2. Describe why schools use filtering, monitoring and an Acceptable Use Agreement.
  3. Follow safe sign-in and organisation routines, and present my work clearly.

Your teacher will guide any school-specific sign-in or account setup. Never share or type your real password into this website.

NEW LEARNING · 1 OF 5

Our three Computing expectations

Respect for others

  • Use SLANT when the teacher is speaking.
  • Listen when others contribute.
  • Support other people's learning.

Respect the classroom

  • Leave desks as you found them.
  • Keep food and drinks away from computers.
  • Follow the Acceptable Use Agreement.

Respect learning

  • Be ready to answer questions and try tasks.
  • Keep folders and Class Notebooks well presented.
  • Keep trying when something is difficult.

The PowerPoint groups our classroom expectations into these three areas.

NEW LEARNING · 2 OF 5

Why do we have an Acceptable Use Agreement?

An Acceptable Use Agreement explains how we must use school computers, networks and accounts responsibly.

Protect people, learning and equipment

Use school systems for schoolwork, respect privacy and follow teacher instructions. The school will explain how to accept its current agreement.

Read the current school agreement with your teacher. Do not assume an older PowerPoint is the current policy.

NEW LEARNING · 3 OF 5

What is filtering?

Filtering can prevent unsuitable websites from opening on the school network or school-managed devices.

Sometimes a suitable page might be blocked. Tell your teacher rather than trying to bypass the filter.

NEW LEARNING · 4 OF 5

What is monitoring?

School systems can be checked

Monitoring means authorised school staff may review activity on school accounts and systems in line with school policies.

Use school computers, browsing and email for schoolwork. If you have a question, ask a teacher.

Specific monitoring arrangements and consequences are governed by the current school policy, not this older slide deck.

NEW LEARNING · 5 OF 5

Safe sign-in and organised work

School account

Follow current school sign-in guidance. Use a long, unique password or passphrase. Never share it.

Folder or notebook

Keep work in order. Add the title and date; use clear headings and labels.

Safe evidence

When asked, add a screenshot of completed work to your Class Notebook — without revealing private details.

The original slide says passwords must have at least 10 characters. Check current account requirements with your teacher; never write your password in a planner.

What details should stay private when using a school account?

Teacher uses a demonstration account or blank example — never pupils' passwords on the classroom screen.

REVEAL EACH STEP

My demonstration

1. Open the school sign-in page selected by your teacher. Check the school or service name before typing.
2. Enter your school username privately. Your username identifies your account; your password proves it is yours.
3. Choose or update a unique password or passphrase only as your teacher instructs. Check current account requirements.
4. Sign in without displaying your password on screen. Do not type it into the Learning Hub, a class chat or your planner.
5. Open the correct work area. Give a piece of work a clear title and date so you can find it again.
WE DO · GUIDED PRACTICE

What should Alex do?

OUR SCENARIO

A friend asks Alex for a school password. Later, a research website does not open because it has been blocked.

Discuss how Alex can keep their account secure and ask for help appropriately.

BUILD THE RESPONSE

Our safe choices

1. Explain why you should not share your password, even with a friend.
2. Remember: a blocked website might be unsuitable, or the filtering decision might need checking.
3. Tell a teacher which page was blocked. Do not try to bypass the school filter.
4. Use your own account, name and date your work, and save it where your teacher directs.
5. Turn & Talk: explain the correct next action. Teacher counts in: 3, 2, 1 — Silence.
TASK 1 · SCHOOL RULES

Review acceptable use

Read or review the current school Acceptable Use Agreement with your teacher. Show you understand the expectations.

1. How do we show respect when others are speaking?
2. Why should we keep food and drink away from computers?
3. What should you do if a website seems wrongly blocked?
4. Why is it important to use school systems for schoolwork?

Complete any agreement or acknowledgement using the school's approved process. This website does not collect an agreement.

Complete practical work only using your approved school account and teacher instructions; this Learning Hub page does not store your responses.

TASK 2 · SCHOOL ACCOUNT

Sign in securely

Follow your teacher's instructions to access your own school account. Keep your credentials private.

1. Check you are on the correct sign-in page.
2. Use your own school username.
3. Use a unique private password or passphrase following current school rules.
4. Ask your teacher for help if access fails.

Never type or show your real password on this Learning Hub page, in a screenshot, or in your planner.

Complete practical work only using your approved school account and teacher instructions; this Learning Hub page does not store your responses.

TASK 3 · PRESENTATION

Organise and present your work

Get ready to keep your Computing work easy to find and easy to read.

1. Put your folder or notebook work in order.
2. Add a clear title and date.
3. Write neatly and use labels for diagrams.
4. Record only the permitted school account details in your planner, as instructed.

The PowerPoint mentions username, school email and printer code. Follow your school's current instructions about what may be recorded. Never record your password.

Complete practical work only using your approved school account and teacher instructions; this Learning Hub page does not store your responses.

TASK 4 · SAFE EVIDENCE

Evidence and reflection

When directed by the teacher, save evidence of your completed work.

1. Open the completed work, not your sign-in screen.
2. Check no password, private email or personal detail is visible.
3. Crop a screenshot so the completed work is easy to see.
4. Add it to your Class Notebook if your teacher requires evidence.
5. Check that your title and date are present.

Finish by sharing a positive example of good learning and one target for next lesson.

Complete practical work only using your approved school account and teacher instructions; this Learning Hub page does not store your responses.
