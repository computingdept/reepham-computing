What does ascending order mean?

ANSWER

Smallest to largest.

QUESTION 2 OF 4

RECALL

Is a list of one item already sorted? Explain.

ANSWER

Yes. There are no other values to be out of order with it.

QUESTION 3 OF 4

APPLY

Merge [2, 7] and [3, 5] into one sorted list.

ANSWER

[2, 3, 5, 7].

QUESTION 4 OF 4

CHALLENGE

Why must both input lists be sorted when you merge by checking only their next values?

ANSWER

Their next values must be the smallest remaining on each side; otherwise a smaller item could be hidden later.

OCR EXAM REFERENCE LANGUAGE

Merge Sort · starter code

array values[8]
array temp[8]
values = [7, 3, 5, 2, 8, 1, 6, 4]

procedure merge(low, mid, high)
    left = low
    right = mid + 1
    out = low

    while left <= mid AND right <= high
        if values[left] <= values[right] then
            temp[out] = values[left]
            left = left + 1
        else
            temp[out] = values[right]
            right = right + 1
        endif
        out = out + 1
    endwhile

    while left <= mid
        temp[out] = values[left]
        left = left + 1
        out = out + 1
    endwhile

    while right <= high
        temp[out] = values[right]
        right = right + 1
        out = out + 1
    endwhile

    for i = low to high
        values[i] = temp[i]
    next i
endprocedure

procedure mergeSort(low, high)
    if low < high then
        mid = (low + high) DIV 2
        mergeSort(low, mid)
        mergeSort(mid + 1, high)
        merge(low, mid, high)
    endif
endprocedure

mergeSort(0, 7)
for i = 0 to 7
    print(values[i])
next i

STEP 1 OF 6

Split the range

Find mid using DIV. Sort low..mid and mid + 1..high. The two ranges do not overlap.

1 of 6
STEP 2 OF 6

Stop splitting

A single item is already sorted. The condition low < high prevents another split for a section of one item.

2 of 6
STEP 3 OF 6

Merge two sorted halves

Set left = low and right = mid + 1. Compare the next available value from each half.

3 of 6
STEP 4 OF 6

Copy the smaller value

Put the smaller value into temp[out]. Advance that side's pointer and the output pointer.

4 of 6
STEP 5 OF 6

Copy leftovers

When one half is exhausted, copy every remaining value from the other half. It is already sorted.

5 of 6
STEP 6 OF 6

Copy the merged range back

Copy temp[low..high] to values[low..high]. The two sorted sections become one sorted section.

Algorithm beside every step
STEP 1 OF 6

Split into halves

[7, 3, 5, 2, 8, 1, 6, 4]
[7, 3, 5, 2]     [8, 1, 6, 4]
STEP 2 OF 6

Split to single items

[7, 3] [5, 2]   [8, 1] [6, 4]
[7] [3] [5] [2] [8] [1] [6] [4]
STEP 3 OF 6

Merge pairs

[3, 7] [2, 5]   [1, 8] [4, 6]
STEP 4 OF 6

Merge the left half

Compare 3 and 2: take 2.
Compare 3 and 5: take 3.
Compare 7 and 5: take 5.
Copy leftover 7.
Result: [2, 3, 5, 7]
STEP 5 OF 6

Merge the right half

Merge [1, 8] with [4, 6].
Take 1, then 4, then 6.
Copy leftover 8.
Result: [1, 4, 6, 8]
STEP 6 OF 6

Final merge

Compare the heads of [2, 3, 5, 7] and [1, 4, 6, 8].
Take: 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7.
Copy leftover 8.
Result: [1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8]

Example 3 of 3
YOUR TURN

Explain the stopping rules

Turn and talk: distinguish the recursive base case from the merge loop stopping.

CHECK THE METHOD

Worked response

mergeSort stops splitting when low >= high.
The main merge loop stops when either half is exhausted.
Leftover loops copy the remaining values.
EXPLAIN WHY

Reasoning

A completed merge is copied back before the next larger merge begins.

👍 Thumb up = True👎 Thumb down = False
